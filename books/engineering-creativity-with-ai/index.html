About this book

ENGINEERING CREATIVITY WITH AI treats creativity as an engineered search-and-validation process rather than a mysterious gift or a one-prompt trick. The book begins by separating novelty from usefulness and by asking what generative AI can actually contribute to creative work. Early chapters examine the cognitive architecture of creative thinking, the strengths and limits of generative models, the division of responsibility between human judgment and machine generation, and the habits required to escape entrenched patterns without mistaking novelty for evidence.

At the center of the book is AI-CE9, a proposed nine-stage creativity-engineering workflow. The sequence moves from discovering the real opportunity and mapping the existing solution space to reframing the problem, generating functionally diverse candidates, transferring ideas across domains, evaluating alternatives, building a minimum viable prototype, validating originality and real-world value, and iterating from evidence. AI-CE9 is presented as an integrative method to be tested, not as a scientifically proven universal law or a guarantee of breakthrough innovation.

The middle chapters expand the creative toolbox beyond ordinary brainstorming. TRIZ, SCAMPER, Systematic Inventive Thinking, morphological analysis, analogical reasoning, inversion, paradox, systems thinking and transformational creativity are used to manipulate problem structure deliberately. These methods are connected to prompt engineering, controlled divergence, multi-agent creative collaboration and constructive dissent so that AI output becomes a structured exploration space rather than a stream of plausible but unexamined suggestions.

Research-grounded creativity receives its own engineering treatment. The book explains how retrieval-augmented generation and search can support source-aware ideation, how multimodal systems can accelerate prototyping, and how experiment design can help distinguish a persuasive concept from a candidate that survives testing. Throughout the manuscript, observed evidence, inference, proposals, validation and unresolved questions are treated as different epistemic states. Illustrative cases are not presented as completed scientific experiments or commercial deployments.

Application chapters carry the framework into product and consumer innovation, literature and media, software and technology invention, scientific hypothesis engineering, and educational and social innovation. The focus is not on automating the creator out of the process. Instead, AI is positioned as a controllable instrument for widening search, exposing alternatives, generating prototypes and criticizing assumptions while humans remain responsible for problem definition, evidence quality, ethics, context and final judgment.

The final part connects creative output to real-world value. It examines economic opportunity, commercialization models, intellectual property, authorship, AI-assisted inventions, responsible innovation and the limits of AI creativity. The book closes by helping readers build a personal AI creativity laboratory and complete a 30-day creativity-engineering challenge. Appendices provide a nine-stage field record, pre-publication review checklist, worked AI-CE9 dossier, empirical evaluation design, creative-engineering prompt patterns and a publishing/reproducibility record. The result is a practical framework for turning AI-assisted ideation into a more disciplined cycle of search, prototype, evidence and revision.

What you will learn

  • Distinguish novelty, usefulness, validation and commercial success instead of treating them as interchangeable measures of creativity.
  • Use generative AI as a search and prototyping instrument while keeping human judgment responsible for evidence, ethics and final decisions.
  • Apply the nine stages of AI-CE9 from opportunity discovery through iteration without presenting the framework as a guaranteed recipe for invention.
  • Map an existing solution space before generating alternatives so an unfamiliar idea is not automatically mistaken for an original one.
  • Reframe problems and constraints deliberately to expose hidden assumptions and new solution directions.
  • Generate functionally diverse candidates through controlled divergence rather than accepting near-duplicate model outputs.
  • Use cross-domain analogy and transformation to transfer useful structures without copying surface features.
  • Evaluate candidate ideas with explicit criteria before investing in prototyping or commercialization.
  • Build minimum viable prototypes whose purpose is to test a claim rather than merely make an idea look convincing.
  • Separate proposed concepts from observed evidence and define what would count as validation for a specific claim.
  • Use TRIZ, SCAMPER, SIT and morphological analysis as structured mechanisms for changing a problem or design.
  • Apply inversion, paradox, systems thinking and transformational creativity to explore conceptual spaces beyond ordinary brainstorming.
  • Write prompts that support original thinking while recognizing that prompt sophistication does not prove originality or value.
  • Use multi-agent collaboration and constructive dissent to expose hidden assumptions and compare competing hypotheses.
  • Ground creative research with search and RAG while preserving source provenance and distinguishing retrieved evidence from generated interpretation.
  • Use multimodal AI to accelerate sketches, diagrams, interfaces and other prototypes without confusing visual plausibility with feasibility.
  • Design experiments and evaluation criteria that can challenge a creative claim instead of merely confirming it.
  • Adapt the framework to products, media, software, scientific hypotheses, education and social innovation.
  • Consider commercialization, intellectual property, authorship and responsible innovation before scaling a promising concept.
  • Build a personal AI creativity laboratory and complete a 30-day practice sequence that converts creative work into inspectable records and repeatable experiments.
