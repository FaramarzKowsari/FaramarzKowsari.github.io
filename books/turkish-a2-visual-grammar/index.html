A complete visual A2 Turkish course in one collection

Turkish A2 Visual Grammar brings Books 6-11 together in a 306-page illustrated course for learners ready to move beyond beginner Turkish. The collection develops deeper noun structures, definite objects, compound nouns, possessive and case chains, comparisons, ability, necessity, obligation, advice, habits and the aorist, then continues into past narratives, time relations, sentence connectors, reasons, conditions, purpose, verbals, relative ideas, reported speech and final A2 consolidation.

The teaching style is visual, cumulative and practical. Turkish forms are broken into roots, suffixes, buffer consonants and personal endings, then rebuilt through short examples, dialogues, mini stories, review charts and challenge-based practice. Later books increasingly combine earlier structures so the learner moves from isolated forms toward connected A2 sentences, short narratives and everyday problem-solving.

Book 6 — Objects, Compounds & Deeper Nouns

Book 6 develops the noun system beyond beginner-level forms. It begins with the definite object case -ı / -i / -u / -ü, contrasting a general noun such as kitap with a specific object such as kitabı. Vowel-final nouns introduce the buffer y, producing forms such as suyu, arabayı and elmayı. The learner also practices object questions such as Kimi? and Neyi?, then builds full subject-object-verb sentences.

The next sequence teaches compound nouns through forms such as Türk kahvesi, okul çantası, telefon numarası and ev kapısı. It distinguishes a compound that names a type from a personal possessive form, then shows how a compound can take another case ending. This leads naturally into possessive + case chains: kitabımda, kitabımı, evime, evimden, odasında and arabasından, including the buffer n that connects possessive endings to following case suffixes.

Location is expanded through içinde, üstünde, altında, yanında, önünde and arkasında. The suffix -ki then turns locations into modifiers, as in evdeki kitap, and is also introduced in ownership forms such as benimki and seninki. The section closes with comparison and superlative patterns using daha and en, special forms such as daha iyi and daha kötü, and an explicit sentence-order pattern built around Time + Place + Object + Verb.

Book 7 — Ability, Necessity & Obligation

Book 7 organizes modal meanings into a practical progression. Ability is introduced with -ebil / -abil, including vowel harmony, person forms, negative ability, and questions such as Gelebilir misin? and Yüzebilir misin?. The same structure is then used for polite permission — Girebilir miyim? — and for possibility, as in Yağmur yağabilir.

The necessity section compares several ways of expressing need. lazım appears in forms such as Gitmem lazım; gerek / gerekiyor expresses that something is necessary; zorunda olmak introduces stronger obligation such as Gitmek zorundayım; and ihtiyacım var / yok expresses having or not having a need. By placing these forms side by side, the course makes their differences visible rather than treating every English “need” or “have to” as the same Turkish structure.

Advice is taught with -malı / -meli, including positive, negative and question forms such as çalışmalısın, yapmamalısın and Ne yapmalıyım?. Suggestions then shift into Hadi gidelim and Sinemaya gidelim mi?, while polite desire is practiced through Bir çay isterim. Airport, clinic, help-desk and team-planning mini stories combine ability, need, obligation, advice, permission and polite requests in situations that resemble everyday communication.

Book 8 — Habits & Aorist

Book 8 introduces the Turkish aorist / geniş zaman as a system for habits, routines, repeated actions and general truths. It explicitly contrasts an action happening now with a recurring habit, helping learners distinguish the present continuous -yor from aorist forms such as gelirim, yaparsın, içer, gideriz and çalışırlar.

The course separates major aorist suffix patterns — -r, -ar / -er and -ır / -ir / -ur / -ür — and practices them across persons and common verbs. It then introduces negative aorist forms such as gelmem, yapmazsın and bilmez, followed by questions such as Gelir misin? and polite request patterns such as Yardım eder misin?.

The later lessons connect the aorist to frequency expressions and real routines: her gün, genellikle, bazen, hiç, sabahları and hafta sonları. It is also used for general truths, preferences, invitations and open promises. A dedicated comparison distinguishes the aorist from the future tense, for example the difference between a general or open Ararım and the more specific planned Arayacağım. Mini stories, family habits, café routines, review charts and a final boss-style review consolidate the material.

Book 9 — Past Continuous & Time Relations

Book 9 develops narrative time. It introduces the past continuous with -yordu, showing how -yor combines with past -du in forms such as okuyordum, yazıyordun, geliyordu and çalışıyorduk. Negative forms such as gelmiyordum and uyumuyordum are followed by questions including Ne yapıyordun? and Geliyor muydun?.

Time relationships are then built through -ken for overlapping actions, -den önce and -den sonra for before/after, -den beri for a starting point that continues to the present, and -dır/-dir for duration. Examples include Okurken müzik dinliyorum, Yemekten önce ellerimi yıkarım, İşten sonra kafeye gidiyorum, Dünden beri hastayım and İki yıldır Türkçe öğreniyorum. The book also distinguishes duration from starting point and adds time markers such as çoktan, henüz, daha, az önce and demin.

The storytelling sequence combines a long background action with a shorter event, as in Kitap okuyordum, kedi geldi, and contrasts past continuous with simple past through okuyordum / okudum. Calendar expressions, önce for “ago,” time ranges such as Saat dokuzdan beşe kadar, mini-stories and a narrative-builder page help learners organize past events into short connected stories.

Book 10 — Clauses, Reasons & Conditions

Book 10 moves from single sentences to connected ideas. It develops reason and result through çünkü and bu yüzden, contrast through ama / fakat, paired connectors such as hem...hem, ne...ne and ya...ya da, and sequence with -ınca / -ince. Examples such as Gelmedim çünkü hastaydım, Hastaydım. Bu yüzden gelmedim, Hem Türkçe hem İngilizce konuşuyorum and Eve gelince ara show how separate ideas become longer A2 sentences.

Conditionals are introduced with -sa / -se in patterns such as Gelirsen sevinirim, Vaktin varsa konuşalım and Hava güzelse parka gideriz. Negative and alternative conditions add değilse and yoksa. The book also introduces accessible expressions of wish, uncertainty and concession through keşke, belki, rağmen and olsa da.

Purpose is expressed with infinitive + için, for example Türkçe öğrenmek için çalışıyorum and Gitmek için hazırlanıyorum. The final part opens the door to embedded clauses and reported ideas with examples such as Nerede olduğunu bilmiyorum, Onun geldiğini biliyorum, Ali geleceğini söyledi, direct speech with dedi, and introductory indirect speech such as Ali geldiğini söyledi. Reason, condition, purpose and sequence dialogues then combine these structures in short communicative scenes.

Book 11 — Verbals, Reported Speech & the A2 Final Quest

Book 11 completes the A2 sequence by showing how Turkish verbs can function inside larger sentence structures. It reviews infinitives and develops verbals through patterns such as Kitap okumayı seviyorum, Yüzmeyi biliyorum, Türkçe öğrenmeye başladım, Çalışmaya devam ediyorum, Gitmeye karar verdim and Anlamaya çalışıyorum. It also adds -mekten hoşlanmak, -meden / -madan, -meden önce, -dikten sonra and -erek / -arak for relations such as enjoying, starting, continuing, deciding, trying, doing without, before/after doing, and doing something by means of another action.

Simple relative ideas are introduced through forms such as gelen adam, gelen öğrenci, okuduğum kitap and gittiğim yer. Subject-clause awareness appears in Gelmen iyi oldu. Reported speech is then consolidated by comparing direct and indirect forms, including “Geliyorum,” dedi, Geleceğini söyledi, Gelip gelmeyeceğini sordu and embedded information such as Nerede olduğunu bilmiyorum. Expressions such as sanıyorum and umarım add common ways to frame thought and hope.

The final chapters deliberately reconnect the whole A1-A2 system: noun cases and possession, present/past/future, aorist, ability and obligation, past continuous and time relations, connectors and clauses, verbals and reported speech. Integrated travel-planning and problem-solving dialogues, a reflective “My Turkish Journey” mini-story, multi-stage review challenges and the final A2 quest turn the last book into a cumulative consolidation of the entire collection.

What learners practice

  • Definite-object marking and the difference between general and specific nouns.
  • Buffer consonants y and n in natural suffix chains.
  • Compound nouns and the difference between type and personal possession.
  • Possessive forms followed by accusative, locative, dative and ablative cases.
  • Location expressions, -ki modifiers, comparisons and natural Turkish word order.
  • Ability, inability, permission and possibility with -ebil / -abil.
  • Necessity, obligation and advice with lazım, gerek, zorunda olmak and -malı / -meli.
  • Aorist forms for routines, habits, general truths, polite requests, preferences and invitations.
  • Past continuous positive, negative and question forms with -yordu.
  • Time relationships with -ken, -den önce, -den sonra, -den beri, duration expressions and kadar.
  • Building short past narratives and distinguishing background actions from completed events.
  • Connecting ideas with reason/result, contrast, paired conjunctions and -ınca/-ince.
  • Conditional sentences with -sa/-se, including negative/alternative conditions with değilse and yoksa.
  • Expressing purpose with infinitive + için and introducing embedded clauses.
  • Using verbals in patterns with sevmek, bilmek, başlamak, devam etmek, karar vermek and çalışmak.
  • Recognizing relative ideas, subject clauses, direct and indirect speech, embedded questions and reported content.
  • Combining grammar in dialogues, mini-stories, problem-solving scenes, review charts and cumulative challenge pages.

Who this book is for

For learners who have completed beginner Turkish and want to make suffix combinations, connected sentence structure, narrative time and everyday modal meanings more automatic. The visual design is especially suited to self-study learners who remember grammar better when they can see a form assembled step by step and then apply it immediately in a sentence, dialogue, story or practical situation.
