Worked Bid × Ask example: delta is not a verdict

A footprint asks a question a normal candlestick cannot answer on its own: where within the bar did aggressive buying and selling occur? The book uses a strict sequence—Context → Location → Order Flow → Trigger → Risk—so the data cannot become a license to trade every colorful imbalance.

Read each row as executed aggression

In a Bid × Ask display, the bid column describes trades classified as market sells hitting bids; the ask column describes market buys lifting offers. The precise classification depends on venue, feed and platform. A single impressive row is not a universal signal.

Compute the example delta explicitly

In an illustrative five-row calculation, the totals are 620 bid-side units and 784 ask-side units. With the convention Delta = Ask − Bid, the net delta is +164. This describes greater aggressive buying volume, not guaranteed price appreciation.

Ask the absorption question

The book defines absorption as large aggressive volume with disappointing price progress. If price remains near resistance despite persistent positive delta, possible passive sell-side absorption deserves investigation. A footprint cannot reveal the identity of a supposed absorbing participant.

Require location and subsequent behavior

Check whether the observation occurs at a relevant session boundary, a prior high/low, or within an established auction. Compare follow-through, acceptance, rejection and a plausible structural invalidation before considering an actual setup.

Paired-reading drill: Imagine the same +164 delta in two contexts. In the first, price advances through a resistance zone and holds above it: the aggressive volume accompanies upward progress. In the second, a high ask-side reading appears repeatedly at the same ceiling but price stalls and then drops: that could support an absorption hypothesis, if later action confirms failure. Delta alone cannot distinguish the scenarios. This is why Chapter 8 places effort beside result.

Practical questions readers ask

Is a positive delta always bullish?

No. It records the imbalance between classified aggressive buy and sell volume. Price response, location, follow-through and market structure determine its interpretation.

Can footprint charts identify the exact large buyer or seller?

No. The book explicitly cautions that absorption may be inferred from effort versus result but the footprint does not disclose a participant's identity.

The original book labels its instructional footprint values as educational reconstructions. The five-row example on this page is separately labeled synthetic and is not presented as verified trade tape.

01

THE ANATOMY OF THE AUCTION

The candle is the headline. The transaction record is the story.

Open, high, low and close describe a completed price bar. They do not reveal how executed volume was distributed across its individual price levels. A footprint offers a closer view: trade volume by price, buyer- and seller-initiated execution, and the difference between apparent effort and actual price progress.

That deeper view comes with limitations. Display settings, exchange or venue coverage, trade classification, historical reconstruction and data aggregation can change the appearance of a chart. A centralized futures feed is not interchangeable with fragmented crypto venues or decentralized spot forex. A color-rich cell also cannot identify the market participant who placed the order.

What to examine: record the contract, feed, tick size and aggregation setting before interpreting a cluster. Then ask whether price accepted or rejected the traded area. 02

BID × ASK AND DELTA

What if aggressive buyers keep winning the prints but not the price?

Bid × Ask volume separates executions at the bid from those at the ask. A buyer who crosses the spread is typically classified as aggressive; a seller who hits the bid is classified likewise on the sell side. Delta is commonly calculated as ask-side executed volume minus bid-side executed volume. It measures classified aggression, not the identity or intention of a participant.

Imagine positive delta building beneath resistance while the market struggles to gain ground. The mismatch raises a question about passive supply and possible absorption. It does not prove that a large seller is present or that the next bar must decline. Conversely, price can advance with modest delta if liquidity is thin or the order book shifts.

What to examine: compare delta with bar range, closing location, the relevant high or low, and the following response. Does the market hold beyond a level or immediately return? 03

IMBALANCE STRUCTURE

An imbalance cluster only becomes interesting when the auction responds.

Volume imbalances draw attention to differences between aggressive buy and sell executions at or around neighboring price levels. Some platforms compare bid and ask diagonally, others employ different ratios, filters or aggregation rules. An imbalance displayed using a 3:1 threshold is a configuration choice, not a universally validated trading signal.

Several consecutive qualifying levels can form a stacked imbalance. This may identify concentrated directional execution, but the location and outcome remain decisive. If price holds above a buy-side cluster and continues, the observation fits an acceptance narrative. If price immediately reverses through the same region, visible aggression failed to secure sustained control.

What to examine: note platform settings, minimum volume, session conditions, nearby structure and subsequent acceptance. Save both continuation and failure cases during market replay. 04

ABSORPTION & EXHAUSTION

Two very different reasons for a market to stop advancing

A market that cannot move higher may be confronting substantial passive offers, or it may simply be running out of aggressive buyers at the extreme. These interpretations should not be merged. Absorption becomes plausible when considerable executed aggression repeatedly fails to produce expected progress. Exhaustion becomes plausible when participation itself appears to diminish near an auction boundary.

Neither pattern automatically predicts a reversal. A high-volume stall can eventually break in the same direction, and a quiet high can be followed by renewed buying. The most informative evidence often arrives afterward: repeated failure, a reclaim, a break in local structure or decisive continuation beyond the level.

What to examine: mark the high, low or liquidity reference before price reaches it. Compare effort with result across successive tests; distinguish declining activity from heavy opposing activity. 05

VALUE & MARKET LOCATION

Candle POC, Volume Profile and VWAP are not interchangeable

The candle point of control is the price level with the most executed volume within a specific bar. A session Volume Profile POC captures the busiest price in a broader distribution. VWAP is a volume-weighted average price calculated over a defined period. Each reference summarizes a different aspect of trading activity.

A heavy-volume level may attract later trading, but that does not establish permanent support or resistance. Likewise, proximity to session VWAP can mean something different in a balanced session than in a volatile directional trend. A footprint imbalance becomes more useful only when its location is judged against the broader auction and the market’s subsequent response.

What to examine: specify every indicator’s calculation window. Compare a bar-level POC with the developing session profile, VWAP and prior market structure. Then identify what price behavior would challenge your interpretation. The Volume Profile and VWAP chapters of Footprint Charts expand this location-first approach.

06

CONFIRMATION & TRADE DESIGN

The best signal may be the footprint setup you decide not to trade.

Strong positive delta above a prior high can accompany a legitimate breakout, a temporary price probe or an unsuccessful attempt that traps late buyers. The footprint cannot settle those possibilities by itself. A disciplined sequence begins with context → location → order flow → trigger → risk.

For a possible failed breakout, watch whether price returns through the level and fails to regain it. For continuation, look for sustained acceptance beyond the reference and a defensible retest. When the auction remains ambiguous, doing nothing is a valid conclusion. The story should be written from evidence available at the time, not retrospectively from the winning screenshot.

What to examine: define the entry trigger, structural invalidation, stop distance, slippage allowance, next auction objective and no-trade condition in advance. Replay both winning and losing cases.
