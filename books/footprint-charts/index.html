About this book

Footprint Charts starts where a conventional candlestick stops. A candle compresses a period into open, high, low and close; a footprint expands the same bar into volume at price so the reader can study where transactions concentrated, which side crossed the spread more aggressively, and whether that effort actually produced price progress.

The book is built around a disciplined hierarchy: Context → Location → Order Flow → Trigger → Risk. Footprint data belongs in the middle of the decision chain rather than being treated as a standalone signal. The reader moves from order-flow mechanics and data-source limitations into bid × ask, delta, imbalances, stacked imbalances, absorption, exhaustion, trapped traders, delta divergence and candle POC, then connects those observations with Volume Profile, VWAP, market structure, liquidity, volatility and news conditions.

Later chapters turn observation into execution: reversals at extremes, continuation and breakout acceptance, failed breakouts, pullbacks and retests, false signals and no-trade decisions, entry and invalidation, stops and targets, slippage, execution reality, replay and backtesting. The recurring discipline is to describe what the market actually printed before assigning motive, and to ask what evidence would make the interpretation wrong.

What you will learn

  • Understand what a footprint adds to OHLC candles and what it still cannot tell you.
  • Read resting versus aggressive execution and interpret bid × ask data without confusing execution with trader identity or intention.
  • Use delta, imbalances and stacked imbalances as evidence inside a larger market context.
  • Recognize absorption, exhaustion, trapped traders and price–order-flow disagreement through effort versus result.
  • Work with candle POC and connect footprint evidence to Volume Profile, VWAP, session context and market structure.
  • Judge order-flow evidence differently across liquidity regimes, volatility conditions and news-driven markets.
  • Build setups for reversals, breakouts, failed breakouts, pullbacks and retests while preserving a no-trade option.
  • Define trigger, invalidation, stop, target and risk before turning an interpretation into a trade.
  • Account for slippage, execution constraints, feed differences and platform classification rules.
  • Use replay, paired examples and backtesting to separate robust reading habits from attractive hindsight patterns.

Who this book is for

For discretionary futures, crypto, forex and short-term traders who already understand basic candlesticks and want a more rigorous way to read executed activity inside the bar without turning colorful order-flow cells into automatic signals.

Data and risk note

The book explicitly treats its reconstructed footprint figures as educational simulations unless stated otherwise. Exact footprint values depend on venue, feed, aggregation and platform classification. More detail can improve diagnosis, but it does not remove uncertainty or make the future certain. Trading leveraged instruments can involve substantial loss, so footprint evidence must remain subordinate to explicit invalidation and risk control.
