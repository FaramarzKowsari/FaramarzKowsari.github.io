About Mastering Fibonacci Trading

A retracement can look persuasive on almost any chart if the swing points are chosen after the move. The difficult part is deciding which price movement matters, whether the market is trending or ranging, and what would prove the initial reading wrong. Mastering Fibonacci Trading approaches that problem as a visual, structured study of price measurement, not an invitation to buy or sell whenever price touches a percentage.

This 322-page guide by Faramarz Kowsari develops Fibonacci analysis for forex, stocks, cryptocurrencies, commodities and indices. It starts from market structure and the selection of meaningful highs and lows, then connects retracement and expansion tools with support and resistance, confluence, entry planning, invalidation, position sizing and execution. Advanced material extends into Fibonacci channels, price-and-time relationships, harmonic ideas, backtesting and keeping a trading journal.

The distinction running through the book is consequential: the chart supplies the evidence; the Fibonacci tool supplies a measurement. Neither supplies a guarantee. A well-drawn 61.8% retracement can still be a poor trade when the larger structure is broken, the target is too close, volatility changes or the stop makes the position too expensive.

Inside the book: from measurement to a defensible trading plan

1. Choose the price swing before choosing the level

The first decision is not whether a 38.2% or 61.8% line has been touched. It is whether the swing high and swing low represent a genuine structural move. Selecting different anchors after seeing the outcome creates attractive but unreliable explanations. The book places range selection, trend context and swing structure ahead of drawing retracements.

2. Understand retracements, extensions and projections

The guide works through commonly used retracement references including 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6% and 88.6%, alongside expansion and projection references such as 127.2%, 138.2%, 161.8%, 200% and 261.8%. The objective is to know what each measurement means in relation to the chosen move, not to assume any particular ratio has predictive power in isolation.

3. Add confluence without manufacturing certainty

Fibonacci becomes more interpretable when compared with a relevant support or resistance area, the current market structure, a higher-timeframe level and available target space. But several markings derived from the same price movement are not necessarily independent confirmation. The book places location, timeframe and structural invalidation beside the measured levels so that a trade can be rejected even when a chart looks visually tidy.

4. Make execution and risk part of the thesis

A possible reaction zone is only the start. Readers encounter the practical relationship between an entry, stop placement, invalidation, position size and target. The material also addresses trade review, journal design and testing, so the trader can distinguish a repeatable process from a persuasive hindsight illustration.

Further subjects in the publisher's description: Fibonacci channels, price-time relationships, harmonic concepts, backtesting, journaling and building a written trading operating system.

What you will learn

  • Identify a meaningful measured swing before drawing Fibonacci retracement or projection levels.
  • Interpret the main retracement ratios without treating any line as an automatic entry signal.
  • Distinguish a pullback measurement from an extension or projected price objective.
  • Combine Fibonacci with market structure, support and resistance, and multi-timeframe context.
  • Define a proposed trade's invalidation and risk before deciding on position size.
  • Use testing, journaling and review to investigate when a Fibonacci idea fails.

Questions serious Fibonacci traders ask

Does a 61.8% retracement mean price will reverse?

No. It is a measured proportion of a selected price move. Price can pass through it without reacting. The significance of the level depends on the quality of the measured swing, market conditions and the decision rules surrounding it.

What is the difference between a retracement and an extension?

A retracement measures how far price has moved back into a prior swing; an extension or projection measures potential movement beyond a reference leg. Neither supplies an executable entry by itself.

Is the material only for forex?

No. The publisher describes applications across forex, equities, crypto, commodities and indices. Each instrument still requires its own volatility, session, spread and execution considerations.

Who should read it?

Traders learning Fibonacci from the beginning and experienced chart readers seeking a clearer framework for confluence, invalidation, risk and evidence-based review.
