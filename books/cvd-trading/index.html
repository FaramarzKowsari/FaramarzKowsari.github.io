About this book

CVD TRADING treats Cumulative Volume Delta as evidence about classified aggressive execution, not as a stand-alone prediction line. The book begins with the mechanics behind the indicator: volume versus delta, how trades are classified, how a cumulative curve is built, why session resets and anchors matter, and why two platforms can show different CVD histories. Price and CVD are then read together so the reader can distinguish confirmation from disagreement and ask the more useful question: what happened to price when aggressive buying or selling actually appeared?

The central chapters develop the order-flow relationships that make CVD valuable in context. They cover effort versus result, confirmation, divergence, absorption, exhaustion, aggression efficiency, failed auctions, market structure, liquidity, support and resistance, balance and range behavior, VWAP, Volume Profile, footprint charts and DOM. Trend continuation, reversals, breakouts, failed breakouts, trapped traders, session opens, anchoring, re-synchronization and multi-horizon CVD are treated as conditional structures rather than automatic signals. Separate sections address futures, crypto, stocks, FX and CFDs, including the important limits created by data provenance, tick reconstruction, aggregation and platform methodology.

The later part of the book turns interpretation into a testable process. It examines failure modes, backtesting, measurement, case studies in bullish and bearish absorption, successful and failed divergence, range absorption, crypto spot-versus-perpetual differences and trapped aggression. The final playbook chapters place context ahead of signal: market and session first, then structure and location, then volatility and liquidity, then CVD evidence, and only after that execution. Risk, invalidation, slippage and data quality remain independent of confidence in the indicator. The goal is to move from watching a line to reading an auction with CVD as one disciplined layer of evidence.

What CVD Can Tell You—and What It Cannot: Inside the Book

Imagine a market breaking toward a familiar resistance level. Transactions accelerate. The CVD line climbs. Aggressive buying appears unmistakable—yet price stops progressing. Is that strength, weakness or simply a change in available liquidity? A candlestick records the outcome. Cumulative Volume Delta offers one partial view of the effort that produced it. The real work begins when those two views disagree.

CVD TRADING by Faramarz Kowsari is built around that distinction. Across 50 chapters, it moves from transaction classification and CVD construction through absorption, divergence, auction structure, instrument-specific data limitations, case studies and a research-minded trading playbook. The pages below offer an illustrated route into five of its most useful questions. The complete book takes each one further with mechanics, counterexamples, diagnostic checklists and practical review procedures.

1. Begin With the Transactions, Not the Indicator Shape

Volume is the amount traded. Delta asks which side initiated more volume against the displayed bid or ask under the chosen classification method. In a common bid/ask-traded formulation, bar delta is ask-traded volume minus bid-traded volume; CVD accumulates those signed differences from a defined starting point. A large total-volume bar can have little net delta, while several smaller bars can build a persistent cumulative imbalance. Neither observation, on its own, tells you where price must go next.

The early chapters of the book explain execution, trade classification, anchoring and session resets before asking the reader to draw a conclusion from a line. That order matters. A chart built from true classified transactions is not interchangeable with one reconstructed from coarse intrabar movement. Even a correct CVD calculation is only as informative as the market, feed, time window and interpretation supporting it.

2. When Aggressive Orders Meet a Wall

A rising CVD can accompany a rising price. But it can also accompany price going almost nowhere. Consider repeated market buys at an established high: aggressive buyers keep lifting offers while price makes progressively less progress. That is consistent with possible absorption by opposing passive supply. Reverse the situation at a low: substantial selling may be met by buyers willing to absorb it. The crucial comparison is effort versus result, not whether the CVD line happens to be green or red.

Chapter 9 examines this mismatch without pretending that CVD reveals the identity or intention of the passive participant. The book asks readers to look at the price boundary, repeated tests, liquidity conditions and what happens next. Absorption can precede rejection; it can also fail when the resting side is exhausted. A hypothesis worth trading needs a visible invalidation condition. That discipline is more useful than assigning a dramatic story to every large print.

3. The Most Interesting Divergence Is Sometimes the One That Fails

Price makes a higher high, but CVD makes a lower high. The picture looks persuasive. Yet a bearish divergence does not create an automatic short. It describes disagreement between price progress and the measured aggressive flow. The meaning changes if the comparison crosses a session reset, if historical data have been reconstructed differently, or if price is approaching a major level rather than wandering through the middle of a balanced range.

The book treats successful and failed divergences separately. In its discussion of failed divergence, price may accept above resistance even after apparent bearish disagreement. If aggressive flow subsequently re-aligns with price, the abandoned reversal idea becomes information about continuation. That is a richer lesson than simply labeling the first mismatch “sell.” The question is what would have to happen for the original interpretation to be wrong—and whether that event has already occurred.

4. Location, Data Provenance and the Auction Around the Line

There is a difference between an imbalance at a well-tested session low and the same-looking imbalance halfway through a featureless overnight drift. Context gives the measurement somewhere to matter. The book places CVD alongside market structure, liquidity zones, VWAP, Volume Profile, footprint charts and the Depth of Market. These tools do different jobs: one describes aggressive executions, another highlights volume-weighted location, another shows distributions of activity or resting quotes. Stacking them without a reason does not create certainty.

This is also why the book devotes chapters to futures, crypto, stocks, FX and CFDs. Exchange-traded futures, crypto spot markets and perpetual contracts can produce different CVD pictures because their venues and transaction records differ. Two platforms may even disagree on the same instrument when one uses bid/ask trade data and another estimates direction from lower-timeframe bars. Rather than choosing whichever curve confirms a preferred theory, the reader learns to document the venue, data feed, classification rule, historical coverage, anchor and session template. That technical honesty can prevent an attractive but unrepeatable observation from becoming a costly decision.

5. A Screenshot Is Not a Strategy. Build Something You Can Test.

A striking historical divergence or absorption pattern is easy to circle after the price move has ended. A usable playbook must define the conditions that would have been known before the move: instrument, session, reference level, CVD condition, price trigger, invalidation, exit logic and likely execution cost. Chapter 35 begins the transition to testable setups; Chapters 36–38 deal with backtesting, measurement and failure modes; the closing chapters put those ideas into a disciplined process.

Consider a hypothetical selling-pressure test at a prior session low. Before imagining the reversal, record the level and feed, then observe whether sell aggression is gaining or losing the ability to extend price. A bounce alone is not sufficient: what is the precise price trigger? What price would disprove the absorption thesis? Could the trade still make sense after spread, commissions and slippage? The book’s case studies intentionally include losing interpretations and failed divergences because a framework that can explain only winning charts has not been tested honestly.

Its diagnostic checklists, glossary, formulas, backtesting worksheet and visual companion appendix help turn scattered observations into a reviewable practice. Measures such as expectancy, drawdown, maximum adverse excursion and maximum favorable excursion place the resulting trade history under a tougher light than a collection of memorable winners.

Four order-flow paradoxes that reward closer reading

A valuable CVD observation often begins where the line and price disagree. These source-based scenarios show why the book separates aggressive execution, the resulting price response, and what the analyst still cannot know.

Price climbs while CVD struggles to confirm

This mismatch may be worth investigating, but its interpretation depends on timeframe, trade classification, liquidity and what happens at the next relevant reference. A contradiction is not a compulsory short.

Aggressive selling hits a low but the market stops moving down

The price response suggests the selling may be absorbed, yet confirming who is providing the other side is more difficult than reading the line. The book carefully distinguishes observation from inference.

Spot CVD and perpetual CVD tell different stories

Two datasets can legitimately classify different executions. The disagreement should send the researcher back to venue, instrument, reset convention and aggregation logic—not automatically toward one winning forecast.

The divergence fails and price keeps trending

The book treats failed divergence as useful evidence about what the original relationship did not establish. Retrospective explanations are not a substitute for fixed decision-time rules.

A CVD diagnostic before acting on a divergence

  1. Declare the instrument, venue and data feed before interpreting the line.
  2. Identify the trade-sign classification method and reset/anchor conditions.
  3. Observe price response relative to aggressive execution, not only curve direction.
  4. Record nearby market structure, liquidity and whether the market is balancing or trending.
  5. Specify a failure condition, execution cost and maximum risk before discussing entry.
  6. Journal both successful and failed divergences using the same original rules.

Answers to common CVD questions

What is Cumulative Volume Delta (CVD)?

CVD accumulates signed trade-volume imbalance according to a classification methodology. It describes an execution flow under specific data conventions, not a direct census of everyone who wants to buy or sell.

What is the difference between CVD divergence and absorption?

Divergence compares the price path with the CVD path; absorption concerns aggressive executions that produce less price movement than expected. Both require context and can fail.

Does CVD prove institutional trading?

No. Executed bid/ask classifications cannot independently establish the identities, private intent or unfilled inventory of market participants.

Why can two platforms show different CVD curves?

Venues, data availability, tick classification, bar aggregation, reset and anchoring rules can differ. Chapters 32–34 specifically address data provenance and platform disagreement.

Does the book compare spot and perpetual crypto CVD?

Yes. Chapter 45 is devoted to a crypto spot-versus-perpetual comparison, in addition to earlier futures and crypto chapters.

Does it include failed case studies?

Yes. A divergence that worked and a divergence that failed occupy separate case studies, and the source develops a broader failure-mode taxonomy.

How many chapters are included?

The manuscript has 50 chapters followed by formula references, diagnostic checklists, a glossary, backtesting materials and a visual companion.

Is CVD enough by itself to enter a trade?

No. The book develops CVD alongside price response, structure, liquidity, execution risk and falsifiable invalidation.
