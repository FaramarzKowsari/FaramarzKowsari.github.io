Descrição pública original do livro

inglês

Wealth Begins Where You Stop Selling Yourself Cheap treats wealth as more than money and asks where a life is being quietly discounted before a price is ever named. It connects undercharging with over-explaining, unclear scope, fear of losing clients, exhausted availability, hidden talent, self-erasure in love, weak boundaries, scattered attention, and the body's record of depletion. The book also insists that apprenticeship is not humiliation and that higher prices must be supported by skill, proof, ethical value, and clean exchange rather than entitlement. Later chapters examine attention as a form of salary, panic in the marketplace, leaving without bitterness, and the difference between becoming expensive in image and accurate in life. A thirty-day repricing plan moves through observing leaks, keeping one boundary, strengthening one proof of skill, and making one cleaner exchange so self-respect becomes visible behavior rather than theory.

Objetivos de aprendizagem publicados

inglês

  • Recognize the nonfinancial ways a person can discount time, talent, attention, body, and presence.
  • Separate healthy apprenticeship from chronic underpricing or self-erasure.
  • Use clearer scope, boundaries, terms, and exchanges instead of fear-driven availability.
  • Support higher fees with stronger craft, proof, testimonials, and easier-to-understand value.
  • Notice how panic, over-explaining, and fear of losing a client weaken bargaining behavior.
  • Apply the same value logic to relationships without turning self-respect into hardness.
  • Treat attention and energy as scarce assets that deserve deliberate allocation.
  • Use a 30-day plan to turn value from an idea into repeated observable behavior.
