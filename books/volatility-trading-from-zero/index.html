About this book

A trader can forecast a large market move and still lose money on a purchased option. A trader can correctly expect a rise in the VIX and still misunderstand the instrument held. VOLATILITY TRADING FROM ZERO begins with this uncomfortable but essential distinction: volatility is about the magnitude and distribution of returns, not simply whether prices rise or fall. The opening chapters build the mathematics of realized volatility, return measurement and time scaling, then connect option premium to implied volatility and the Greeks. This foundation explains why market direction, option price, the passage of time and changes in volatility expectations can disagree with one another. The objective is to understand what a position actually owns before predicting what an index might do.

Across 44 chapters, the book opens up the VIX family with care. It explains what the VIX measures, the logic of its calculation, why arbitrary 'high' or 'low' VIX thresholds can mislead, and how VIX1D, VIX9D and VIX3M refer to different horizons. Implied volatility is developed as a model-based price convention rather than a literal forecast or guaranteed probability. The reader then encounters expected moves, IV Rank and Percentile, scheduled catalysts, post-event volatility crush, and the way surprises affect position P&L. The treatment of skew and volatility surfaces goes beyond noticing a smile: different strikes can imply different volatility, and the relative pricing of wings, crash protection and tails changes what a proposed strategy costs.

The next layer separates two curves that are often confused: the term structure of option-implied volatility and the futures curve of the VIX. Their shapes carry different meanings and cannot be traded as if they were interchangeable. Chapters on contango, backwardation, basis, roll mechanics, settlement and the Special Opening Quotation explain why owning a volatility-linked product is not the same as owning the spot VIX level on a chart. The volatility risk premium is defined with attention to realized-versus-implied comparisons and the compensation sought by sellers of insurance-like exposures. Short-volatility carry can look attractive in ordinary conditions while concentrating losses in the left tail; the book examines when that tradeoff deteriorates and why path dependence matters for volatility exchange-traded products.

Readers then move through the actual instruments and expressions of a volatility view: SPX and XSP options, standard and mini VIX futures, VIX index options versus options on VIX futures, and exchange-traded volatility products. Strategy chapters investigate long straddles and strangles, defined-risk short-volatility structures, calendars and diagonals, delta-hedged gamma scalping, VIX-futures curve spreads, tail hedges, and event or 0DTE volatility decisions. Each strategy is framed as an investigation of payoff, exposure, costs, liquidity, execution and what would make the premise invalid. There is particular attention to contracts, expiry and settlement because similar-looking symbols can represent materially different exposures.

The final chapters ask how much volatility risk a portfolio can absorb, how to backtest without looking into the future and how to write a decision playbook that permits a defensible no-trade outcome. Hypothetical calculations are identified as illustrations, not invented performance records. Rather than offering a signal to buy whenever the VIX crosses a number, the book teaches readers to ask whether uncertainty is already expensive, which horizon matters, what the instrument tracks, how the position may fail, and whether the evidence justifies risking capital. That is the practical meaning of learning volatility trading from zero.

What you will learn

  • Separate a directional market forecast from a forecast about the size and distribution of returns.
  • Measure realized volatility and explain how time horizon and annualization affect comparisons.
  • Understand why option prices can lose value despite an accurate move forecast, using implied volatility, theta, delta, gamma and vega.
  • Interpret the VIX family as horizon-specific measures rather than market-direction forecasts or universal buy/sell thresholds.
  • Compare IV Rank, IV Percentile, expected moves and scheduled-event pricing without treating model conventions as guarantees.
  • Read volatility skew, put wings, tail pricing and the surface as relative prices of different strike-and-expiry risks.
  • Distinguish an options volatility term structure from the VIX futures curve, including basis and settlement effects.
  • Explain volatility risk premium, short-volatility carry, left-tail risk and the conditions under which carry breaks down.
  • Select an appropriate instrument among SPX/XSP options, VIX futures, VIX options and volatility ETPs while respecting their different mechanics.
  • Evaluate straddles, strangles, spreads, calendars, gamma scalping, curve trades and tail hedges through their actual exposures.
  • Account for liquidity, order execution, position size, portfolio concentration, roll mechanics and path dependence.
  • Design a backtest without look-ahead data, label illustrative numbers correctly and document failure cases alongside profitable trades.
  • Build a decision playbook that explicitly includes abstention when the instrument, price or risk budget does not justify a trade.

Who this book is for

For curious beginners who understand basic market terminology, options students, index traders, futures traders, systematic researchers and experienced directional traders who want to understand why volatility exposure behaves differently from owning the underlying asset. The sequence is useful for readers progressing from basic returns and option pricing toward VIX products, volatility structures, portfolio hedging and defensible research. Some chapters require comfort with options, probabilities and derivatives mechanics; the early chapters establish the necessary language. All strategies involve risk and are discussed for education, not as individualized investment advice.
