About this book

The Discipline of Bad Days is about continuing meaningful work without pretending that every difficult day is ordinary. It distinguishes emergencies and genuine exhaustion from resistance, then replaces the demand for perfect motivation with smaller, humane minimum actions, return rituals, clean boundaries, rest that does not become desertion, and systems that reduce dependence on mood. Across forty-two chapters and a field manual, the book treats consistency as the ability to return rather than the ability to perform at maximum intensity every day.

What you will learn

  • Distinguish a hard day from an emergency, exhaustion, or simple resistance.
  • Scale work down without abandoning it completely when capacity is reduced.
  • Use minimum actions, return rituals, logs, and environment design to lower friction.
  • Rest without converting recovery into indefinite avoidance.
  • Build consistency around returning to the work rather than waiting for the right feeling.

Key topics

  • Bad-day discipline
  • Minimum viable action
  • Resistance and exhaustion
  • Return rituals
  • Energy and workspace
  • Rest versus avoidance
  • Boundaries
  • Consistency without self-violence

Who this book is for

For writers, creators, professionals, students, and anyone who wants a humane system for continuing through low-motivation or difficult days.
