Description publique originale du livre

anglais

SILVER TRADING is an evidence-led practical guide that connects the physical and industrial silver market with the instruments, price behavior, execution mechanics and risk decisions faced by active traders. It begins by separating silver from the simplified idea of being merely a cheaper form of gold. The book examines supply, recycling, industrial fabrication, inventories, macroeconomic forces, the gold-silver ratio and the global trading network, then asks a more important practical question: what exactly is being traded when a trader sees XAGUSD, a CFD, a futures contract, a metal-linked product or physical exposure on a screen? The emphasis throughout is on matching the evidence to the instrument and time horizon rather than using a long-term narrative to justify an unrelated intraday position. The market-analysis sections move from data quality and silver-specific volatility into trends, ranges, failed breakouts and multi-timeframe structure before applying Smart Money Concepts with explicit limits on inference. Liquidity pools, market-structure shifts, Fair Value Gaps, Order Blocks, premium-discount zones, reclaims and trapped-position setups are treated as observable frameworks that require defined rules, context and invalidation rather than as proof of hidden institutional intent. Strategy chapters then organize those ideas into trend continuation, pullback, range rotation, mean reversion, failed-break, macro-event and cross-market confirmation workflows, while repeatedly separating what can be observed from what remains only an interpretation. The execution and risk sections translate a silver quote into real contract exposure. Contract multipliers, futures expiration, rollover, settlement, MCX silver trading, currency effects, leverage, margin, slippage…

Objectifs d'apprentissage publiés

anglais

  • Understand why silver combines precious-metal and industrial-market behavior and why that makes its trading logic different from simply copying a gold strategy.
  • Distinguish physical exposure, XAGUSD or CFD exposure, exchange-traded futures and other silver instruments before applying a trading thesis.
  • Connect supply, recycling, industrial demand, inventories, macroeconomic conditions and the gold-silver ratio to the correct analytical horizon without turning long-term narratives into automatic short-term signals.
  • Build silver charts and market observations with attention to data provenance, quote source, timeframe and the limitations of monthly or benchmark averages for intraday execution.
  • Classify trends, ranges and failed breakouts with reproducible rules and evaluate them across different volatility conditions rather than selecting only visually persuasive examples.
  • Apply liquidity, market-structure shifts, Fair Value Gaps, Order Blocks, premium-discount zones and reclaims to silver while keeping observable price behavior separate from claims about hidden motives.
  • Construct trend-continuation, pullback, range-rotation, mean-reversion and failed-break frameworks with explicit entry, cancellation and invalidation logic.
  • Translate a silver price move into actual cash exposure using the relevant contract multiplier and distinguish notional exposure, margin collateral and maximum tolerable account risk.
