About this book

Supply and Demand Zones treats a zone as a structured hypothesis about an area of prior imbalance, not as proof of hidden institutional orders and not as a signal by itself. The book begins with auction logic, the four basic zone structures, base quality, proximal and distal boundaries, departure strength, freshness, mitigation and consumption.

The method then connects zone quality to market structure, liquidity, top-down mapping, nested zones, timeframe conflicts, limit versus confirmation entries, invalidation, targets and position sizing. Compression, failed zones, flipped zones and no-trade conditions are given the same attention as successful examples. The final sections turn drawing rules into a backtest, journal and personal playbook.

What you will learn

  • Draw supply and demand zones with consistent proximal and distal boundary rules.
  • Evaluate the base and the departure instead of treating every consolidation as a valid zone.
  • Distinguish fresh, tested, mitigated and consumed zones.
  • Grade zone quality using location, departure strength, structure, available room and higher-timeframe context.
  • Connect higher-timeframe zones with lower-timeframe execution without changing the thesis after the fact.
  • Compare limit entries with confirmation-based entries and define invalidation before participation.
  • Recognize compression, failed zones, flipped zones and situations where the correct action is no trade.
  • Backtest fixed zone definitions chronologically and review decisions in a structured journal.
