About this book

Inverse Fair Value Gaps (iFVG) starts with ordinary Fair Value Gap geometry and asks what changes when the original imbalance loses its role and price establishes itself on the opposite side. The useful information is the failed expectation, not merely a rectangle changing color. The book defines bullish and bearish iFVGs, then connects inversion to market structure, liquidity sweeps, displacement, premium/discount and dealing-range location.

Multi-timeframe analysis leads into three practical entry models, stops, invalidation, targets, risk and confluence without indicator overload. Four market environments, false iFVGs, failure modes, backtesting, a complete process and eight case studies keep the framework testable. FVG, iFVG, MSS and BOS are treated as practitioner terms whose exact conventions must be stated rather than assumed universal.

16 original chapters · Faramarz Kowsari

A failed gap is a new hypothesis, not an automatic reversal signal.

The book follows an imbalance through its complete lifecycle: first identify the original three-candle FVG, then define what failure or inversion would mean, and only afterward assess whether the new role matters in market context. It resists the temptation to transform every breached rectangle into hindsight confirmation. Execution, invalidation, location, data limitations and failure cases remain part of the reading process.

Inside the book: a source-based learning route

These pathways explain how the material develops. The chapters and topics follow the manuscript rather than a collection of generic trading terms.

Chapters 1–4: From the original gap to an inversion

Establish basic FVG geometry, describe the event that turns a previous imbalance into an inverse FVG, and compare the bullish and bearish forms without assuming that every touch is a trade.

Chapters 5–8: Put the inverse zone in context

Evaluate market structure, liquidity sweeps, displacement, premium and discount, dealing ranges and multi-timeframe structure before drawing conclusions from a local rectangle.

Chapters 9–10: Build a defensible entry and exit

The book presents three practical entry models, then examines stop placement, invalidation, objective selection and risk when the price does something different from the plan.

Chapters 11–13: Avoid decorative confluence

Compare useful context with overloaded charts, four iFVG trading environments and false inverse gaps. Learn why a clear shape can still make a bad trade.

Chapters 14–16: Test the interpretation

Backtest without allowing future candles to rewrite earlier labels, formulate a complete iFVG process and learn from eight full case studies including situations that fail.

Where the method meets a difficult chart

The zone is touched but never invalidated

A price test is not necessarily proof that the original FVG failed. The qualifying transition has to be defined first.

The iFVG retest appears against higher-timeframe resistance

A locally recognizable inverse gap can conflict with broader price structure, making the apparent precision misleading.

An attractive reconstruction hides a bad stop

If the original hypothesis has no clear failure condition, an elegant chart offers little practical discipline.

Practical decision checklist

  1. Define the active context before selecting the apparent setup.
  2. Choose only levels or signals that fit the described conditions.
  3. Identify what the next market observation would confirm or contradict.
  4. Write the exact invalidation and defensible next objective.
  5. Check transaction costs, exposure and execution risks.
  6. Record false signals and no-trade cases as carefully as successful reactions.

Questions before buying

How does an iFVG differ from an ordinary FVG?

The concept concerns a previously defined fair value gap whose role changes after a qualifying failure or transition, not just a newly highlighted imbalance.

Does the guide include bullish and bearish setups?

Yes. Chapter 4 distinguishes both inversion directions, and later sections discuss context and execution.

How many entry models are discussed?

Three practical iFVG entry models are the focus of Chapter 9.

Does it teach backtesting?

Yes. The book has an explicit chapter on avoiding hindsight errors and closes with eight case studies.

Are the charts real historical trade records?

The source calls its illustrative charts synthetic educational examples unless specifically labeled otherwise; they do not establish historical strategy performance.
