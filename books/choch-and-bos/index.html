CHoCH & BOS: read the shift, not just the label

A practical guide to Break of Structure, Change of Character, and potential trend reversals. A chart may display dozens of highs, lows, breakouts, and pullbacks. Only some of them belong to the structural story a trader is actually testing. This book starts where most diagram-based explanations stop: with rules for selecting meaningful swings before the future price path is visible.

CHoCH & BOS: Reading Market Structure Shifts, by Faramarz Kowsari, develops a consistent language for distinguishing structural continuation, damage to an existing trend, and the uncertain interval between them. A bullish candle is not automatically a bullish CHoCH. A new high is not automatically a BOS. The interpretation depends on the previous state, the swing that protected it, the timeframe being studied, and the precise break condition declared in advance.

The book then tests those definitions against wick penetrations, candle closes, failed acceptance, liquidity sweeps, nested timeframes, unclear ranges, replay exercises, and algorithmic detection. It treats a structural event as evidence for a hypothesis, not an instruction to enter a trade. When the later outcome disappoints, the original event stays in the journal rather than being renamed after the fact.

The organizing principle: distinguish what price did, what the analyst inferred, what a strategy allows, and what happened afterward. Those four questions should not be answered with one acronym.

CHoCH, BOS, and MSS: operational definitions

These are the conventions used in this book. Trading communities may use the same terms differently, especially when discussing wicks, candle closes, displacement, and the line between CHoCH and MSS.

BOS — Break of Structure
A close beyond a meaningful swing in the direction of an already established structural trend. Bullish continuation breaks a relevant swing high; bearish continuation breaks a relevant swing low.
CHoCH — Change of Character
The first meaningful opposite-side violation of protected structure. It shows that the previous state has been damaged, but does not establish a new trend by itself.
MSS — Market Structure Shift
A stronger transition claim. The book reserves the term for a CHoCH accompanied by additional evidence that an opposite directional state is organizing, such as displacement and later swing development.
Protected swing
The specific structural low whose survival preserves a bullish state, or the structural high whose survival preserves a bearish state. A merely recent pivot is not necessarily the protected pivot.
Internal vs external structure
Two nested swing hierarchies. A fast internal shift may occur within a slower external trend; neither statement is contradictory when the timeframe and pivot rule are explicit.
Sweep, break, and reclaim
A wick probe and rejection, a rule-valid close beyond a level, and a later return through that level are distinct events. A subsequent reclaim does not erase a valid earlier break.

One practical consequence is especially useful: a CHoCH is evidence of transition; a BOS is evidence of continuation. Neither guarantees the next move, produces an entry automatically, or proves why particular market participants acted.

What this book teaches you to do

01 · Read

Select swings without hindsight

Distinguish candidate pivots from confirmed pivots, choose swing sensitivity, handle equal highs and lows with tolerance, and record when a pivot became knowable.

02 · Classify

Separate continuation from transition

Identify the protected reference level and use the previous state to decide whether a confirmed close is BOS, CHoCH, or neither.

03 · Contextualize

Read the same event across scales

Combine internal and external structure with location, opposing levels, volatility, displacement, and multi-timeframe conflicts without changing the original event label.

04 · Evaluate

Recognize false breaks and sweeps

Keep wick penetration, close acceptance, follow-through, rejection, and reclaim in sequence, including cases where the anticipated reversal never follows.

05 · Plan

Build a falsifiable trade hypothesis

Specify invalidation before position sizing, distinguish entry location from entry trigger, and preserve the option to skip a rule-valid but unattractive setup.

06 · Test

Design reproducible structure research

Use state machines, non-repainting confirmation, event logging, simple pseudocode, unit tests, and blind chart replay to reduce look-ahead and selection bias.

Learn from charts that do not tell a perfect story

Chapter 14 includes ten case types, not a selection of guaranteed setups. The exercises deliberately preserve uncertain, failed, and unattractive conditions.

  • Transition in both directions: a bearish sequence loses control, or a bullish protected swing is violated. A change in structural evidence is not the same thing as a timely entry.
  • Continuation in both directions: bullish and bearish BOS examples show why the pre-existing trend determines the label, even when the price has already moved far.
  • Break versus acceptance: a penetration without a qualifying close, a valid break followed by reclaim, and a probe of clustered highs call for separate records.
  • Nested and ambiguous structure: an internal reversal may leave the external structure untouched; a ranging market may create repeated labels without useful directional clarity.
  • A structural event with no trade: the setup may be valid but too close to opposing structure, too volatile, or too far from sensible invalidation.

How to use the cases: hide the right-hand part of the chart, write the structural state and protected swing first, reveal one bar or small block at a time, and compare your original classification with the later annotated explanation. The skill being trained is reasoning under incomplete information, not recognizing a profitable chart after the move.

The Market Structure Field Manual: an eight-step reading process

The appendix turns the method into a concise, repeatable sequence that can be used in discretionary review or translated into code.

  1. Declare the anchor

    Choose the timeframe, feed, and swing-confirmation rule before seeing the event.

  2. Confirm the swings

    Mark pivot candidates separately and do not use a pivot before its right-side confirmation exists.

  3. Define external structure

    Record bullish, bearish, range, or unclear, and name the swing that protects it.

  4. Locate internal structure

    Identify local swing changes without silently promoting them into external reversals.

  5. Classify the event

    Test the wick, close, tolerance, and direction against the rules: BOS, CHoCH, sweep, reclaim, or no event.

  6. Measure the context

    Record opposing levels, structural location, volatility, displacement, and distance to invalidation.

  7. Decide or abstain

    Only then define a trade hypothesis, cancellation condition, position risk, or an explicit no-trade decision.

  8. Preserve the record

    Log what was known at the time; add the next event and eventual outcome without rewriting earlier labels.

Research insight: the book distinguishes a valid but losing decision from a rule violation and from a model whose assumptions need revision. It also proposes descriptive measures such as classification stability, failed-break frequency, reclaim timing, and disagreement between structural scales. None of those measures by itself establishes a profitable strategy.

Three examples of what the labels actually mean

A bullish BOS is conditional on the past

Imagine an established bullish external sequence with a confirmed higher low that still protects the trend. A close beyond the relevant prior swing high is a continuation-side BOS under the book's rule. An immediate move back below the level is logged as a later reclaim, not used to erase the original event.

A CHoCH warns; it does not finish the reversal

In a bearish structure, price first probes the protected lower high with a wick but closes below it. Under a close-based convention, that probe is not a CHoCH. A later qualifying close above the protected high changes the structural question, yet an enduring bullish sequence still requires further evidence.

Two timeframes can disagree without error

A fast bullish CHoCH may interrupt a short-term decline while the larger bearish external protected high remains untouched. The local event is meaningful at its own scale; claiming a confirmed higher-timeframe reversal would go beyond the evidence.

The best classification can still be no trade

A clean breakout may occur immediately beneath a higher-timeframe opposing swing, with volatility-expanded risk and little room for a defensible target. The structural label can be correct while the execution choice is to stand aside.

These are explanatory scenarios based on the book's rules, not present-day trade signals or performance forecasts.

Frequently asked questions

Is a CHoCH proof that the market has reversed?

No. The book defines CHoCH as the first meaningful opposite-side violation of protected structure. It warns of transition; later price action determines whether a new trend develops.

What is the difference between BOS and CHoCH?

Under the book's stated rules, BOS is a continuation-side close beyond a meaningful swing in an established direction. CHoCH is an opposite-side violation of the swing protecting the prevailing state.

Does a wick beyond a swing level count as BOS?

Not under the book's principal close-based convention. A wick-only penetration can be logged as a probe or sweep; a close-based break requires the predefined closing condition.

Can an internal CHoCH happen without a higher-timeframe reversal?

Yes. Internal and external structure describe different swing scales. A local transition can occur while the higher-timeframe protected level remains intact.

Does the book teach algorithmic detection?

Yes. Chapter 13 covers deterministic pivot rules, confirmation timing, state machines, non-repainting records, unit tests, and explicit handling of unresolved states.

Are the chart examples based on real market data?

The book states that Figures 1–28 and 31–34 use historical daily GOOG OHLCV distributed with Matplotlib; Figures 29, 30, 35, and 36 are clearly identified as schematic diagrams.

Who should read this book?

Written for readers who already encounter market-structure language but want to know which parts are observable and which parts are interpretation. It is particularly relevant to Smart Money Concepts (SMC) and ICT-style price-action learners, discretionary chart analysts, trading educators, and researchers developing structure-detection indicators or backtests.

Beginners can follow the early chapters' definitions and diagrams; experienced readers can use the casebook and field manual to examine the consistency of their existing process. The book does not claim that learning CHoCH and BOS creates a trading edge on its own, and it does not replace individualized financial advice.
