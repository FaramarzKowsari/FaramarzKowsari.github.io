About this book

Algorithmic Time & Price Grids develops a disciplined framework for reading intraday markets as a coordinate system in which time and price must be evaluated together. Instead of treating a level as meaningful in isolation, the book asks the reader to define a measured range, identify the relevant time window, locate the price-delivery structure that appears at their intersection, and state in advance what would invalidate the idea. The central operating sequence is simple but demanding: time defines when to look, the measured range defines where to look, the PD Array defines what to look for, and observed price behavior determines whether the hypothesis survives.

The book builds this framework through quadrants and octants, opening prices and Opening Range Gaps, measured expansion, macro windows, nested time grids, higher-time-frame spatial references, Fair Value Gaps and Inversion FVGs, Order Blocks, Breakers, Consequent Encroachment, wick/body logic, efficient delivery, low-resistance liquidity runs, and time-price intersection entries. It then connects higher-time-frame narrative to one-minute execution and examines post-macro continuation, invalidation, risk, false precision, case-study analysis, and repeatable playbook construction. Practical appendices provide the calculation logic for quadrants, octants, opening-range projections and Consequent Encroachment, together with pre-market, intraday and post-trade checklists.

The treatment is intentionally evidence-conscious. ICT/SMC terminology is used as a framework for education, mapping and testing rather than as proof that markets follow a hidden deterministic script. Controlled reconstructions are presented as teaching devices rather than evidence of predictability, and no grid, time window, PD Array or chart pattern is presented as a guaranteed trading edge. The emphasis is on prospective definitions, fixed measurement rules, explicit invalidation, execution constraints, risk awareness, and testing the framework instead of believing it on faith.

From the original 21-chapter manuscript · Faramarz Kowsari

Time tells you when to pay attention. Price gives you a measurable location.

Most charts emphasize horizontal price levels and treat the clock as a label. This book adds a second coordinate: the selected time window. It asks the trader to fix the measured range, compute its quadrants and octants, identify an actual time interval, and then observe how a relevant price-delivery structure behaves at the intersection. That system is conditional, not a belief that markets obey a deterministic hidden clock.

The manuscript separates calculable geometry from interpretation. Opening Range Gaps, PD Arrays, Fair Value Gaps, Inversion FVGs, Suspension Blocks, Consequent Encroachment and macro windows are situated inside an explicitly chosen coordinate system. By the end, the method becomes a hypothesis to backtest, invalidate and revise rather than a chart full of impressive lines.

Three mistakes the book helps you recognize

A beautiful octant drawn from a bad range

Correct arithmetic cannot rescue arbitrary endpoints. The range's relevance must be declared before fine subdivision is useful.

A macro window without a qualifying market event

Time is an observation filter, not permission to enter. The price response must still satisfy structure and invalidation requirements.

A replay that makes the grid look inevitable

If anchor choices and time windows are changed after the move, a clean retrospective example is not a fair test. The book insists on rule freezing and changed assumptions being recorded.

Before using the next chart: six practical questions

  1. What dataset, interval, session definition and range or anchor are in use?
  2. What measurement is objectively known, and what is an interpretation?
  3. What other market state could make the apparent pattern misleading?
  4. What observation would invalidate the hypothesis?
  5. Are execution costs and risk acceptable for the planned trade?
  6. Could this setup be tested without selecting winners after the fact?

What readers ask about the book

Is this an algorithmic trading codebook?

The title refers to structured time-price mapping and repeatable analytical grids; the core manuscript is a discretionary, rule-based research framework, not a promise of a turnkey automated bot.

What is the difference between quadrants and octants?

Both subdivide a selected price interval; quadrants offer broader regions, while octants add finer resolution. The source warns against false precision.

Are session openings and macro windows covered?

Yes. The time-axis chapters analyze opening prices, Opening Range Gaps, macro windows and nested time grids.

Does the book teach how to backtest the concept?

Yes. The final part explicitly treats grid setups as hypotheses to test and highlights curve-fitting, falsification and change control.

Is the approach proven to predict market prices?

No. The author explicitly distinguishes a conditional charting method from claims that markets obey a hidden deterministic script.

Are the chart examples historical trades?

Illustrative reconstruction charts are synthetic unless explicitly labeled as historical with enough source information.

What you will learn

  • Treat time as a trading coordinate rather than a decorative session label and combine it with a prospectively defined price range.
  • Calculate and interpret quadrant and octant levels from fixed endpoints without moving the measurement after the outcome is known.
  • Map opening prices, Opening Range Gaps and measured projections while separating geometric levels from statistical claims.
  • Place PD Arrays such as FVGs, Inversion FVGs, Order Blocks and Breakers inside a time-price context instead of treating patterns as standalone signals.
  • Use Consequent Encroachment, wick/body behavior and first-utilization logic as conditional evidence rather than automatic entry triggers.
  • Build a higher-time-frame narrative and connect it to intraday observation windows and lower-time-frame execution.
  • Define invalidation, no-trade conditions, maximum loss and execution constraints before evaluating potential reward.
  • Test time-price hypotheses with fixed definitions, documented sessions and post-trade review instead of relying on retrospective chart precision.
