About the Book: What Happens Beyond a Gold Candlestick?

A gold futures breakout can look decisive on a chart while the order book tells a less comfortable story. The bid–ask spread may be widening. Visible depth may have vanished as the market approaches the level. Aggressive trades may be consuming liquidity without generating the expected continuation. Or a resting order that looked attractive in hindsight may never have been filled.

GOLD FUTURES: BEYOND PRICE ACTION is built for readers who already understand the basics of futures and chart reading and want to investigate COMEX Gold Futures (GC) and Micro Gold Futures (MGC) at the level where market evidence meets real execution. Rather than retelling general technical analysis, it connects CME Globex auction mechanics, order-flow observations, market microstructure and disciplined replay research to a practical decision process.

The progression is deliberate. First, the reader learns what the data actually represent: trades versus quotes, Market by Price (MBP) versus Market by Order (MBO), aggressor-side classification and the limits of anonymous order-level information. Then come the harder questions: when does displayed depth survive a test, what counts as absorption rather than coincidence, why does a sweep sometimes fail, and how should a trader react when delta, footprint, price, VWAP and auction structure disagree?

From there, the book moves into the distinctive timing and contract mechanics of gold: Asian, London and New York sessions; US macroeconomic releases; contract rolls; the distinction between futures and spot gold; and shifting liquidity conditions. It closes the loop with order types, queue priority, adverse selection, MGC execution, realistic slippage accounting, historical market replay, five conditional trading playbooks and an auditable 30-session practice plan.

The operating principle: Observe the evidence available at the decision time. Separate observation from inference. Test more than one explanation. Choose an executable order—or make a documented decision not to trade.

Why This Is Not Another Generic Price-Action Book

Contract-specific, not chart-generic

GC and MGC differ in contract size, tick value and execution environment. The book treats the actual instrument—not a gold-looking line on a screen—as the starting point for risk.

Evidence instead of institutional mythology

Anonymous order IDs are not trader identities. Pulling, stacking, replenishment and suspected icebergs remain hypotheses with alternative explanations, not guaranteed windows into hidden intentions.

Execution is part of the strategy

Spread, queue position, order type, fill probability, slippage and adverse selection appear before any claim that a setup is tradable.

Historical testing without invented fills

Market replay is framed as reconstruction, blind decision-making and a traceable research record—not a slideshow of profitable hindsight examples.

Five Gold Futures Execution Playbooks

These are conditional research and execution frameworks, not automatic buy or sell signals. Each requires available-at-time evidence, an alternative explanation, a practical order choice and a reason to stand aside when conditions are poor.

01 · New York Opening Drive

Assess early-session initiative, liquidity and acceptance after the open rather than reacting to the first strong candle alone.

02 · Absorption Reversal

Look for aggressive activity with limited progress, then demand structural confirmation before considering reversal execution.

03 · Failed Breakout and Return to Value

Distinguish a failed auction from a brief pause and locate entry, invalidation and realistic fill constraints.

04 · Order-Flow Trend Pullback

Consider whether a pullback preserves initiative and whether the proposed limit or market order makes economic sense.

05 · Post-News Repricing and Patience Entry

Prioritize stabilized data, renewed liquidity and executable conditions after an event-driven price jump.

The Market Replay Laboratory: Practice Without Hindsight

One of the book's central features is a research-oriented replay workflow. Historical data must be selected and licensed appropriately; book events must be reconstructed consistently; gaps and anomalies must be flagged before interpreting a setup. In blind replay, the trader sees only information available up to the decision point and records the proposed action before inspecting what happened next.

Fill assumptions deserve the same scrutiny as directional ideas. A touched limit price is not proof of execution. Queue priority, spread, cancellations, delayed updates and adverse selection may change the result substantially. The book consequently couples replay decisions with a research log and cost-aware evaluation.

30-session practice program: Contract familiarization, feed checks, blind replay, fill and slippage sensitivity, playbook rejection and final review. Success is measured by reproducible reasoning and risk discipline, including correct abstentions—not by a promised profit target.

GC vs MGC: The Contract Matters

GC · Gold Futures

100 troy ounces per contract. With a $0.10/oz minimum price fluctuation, the standard tick is $10 per contract. Contract size, exposure and live market depth must be checked before placing orders.

MGC · Micro Gold Futures

10 troy ounces per contract. With a $0.10/oz minimum price fluctuation, the standard tick is $1 per contract. Smaller notional exposure does not eliminate execution friction, leverage or delivery-related considerations.

Specifications above reflect the contract reference in the book. Confirm the latest contract rules, tick conventions, margin, trading hours, delivery schedule and broker cutoffs with CME Group before trading.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this book about gold spot charts or COMEX futures?

The book centers on COMEX GC and MGC futures contracts and the evidence available from futures trades, quotes and order-book activity. It distinguishes those contracts from spot gold, CFDs and other gold products.

Can I use GC order-flow observations to execute with MGC?

The book examines this workflow, including the need to verify the executable MGC market, spread, depth, tick value, order type, position size and slippage rather than assuming that a GC signal can be copied without adjustment.

Does the book include market replay and practice exercises?

Yes. Dedicated chapters address historical-data selection, event-by-event order-book reconstruction, blind replay, realistic fill simulation, audit logs and a structured 30-session replay program.

Does order flow reveal exactly what institutional traders are doing?

No. Market by Order can convey anonymous displayed order-level information, not participant identities, hidden motives or every form of liquidity. The book emphasizes data provenance, alternative explanations and uncertainty.

Does it promise a profitable gold futures strategy?

No. It develops testable execution hypotheses and research routines, including no-trade decisions, cost accounting and operational risk. Futures trading can produce substantial losses.
