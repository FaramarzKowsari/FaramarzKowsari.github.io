About this book

Liquidity Sweep treats liquidity first as a visible location problem rather than a claim about invisible actors. Prior highs and lows, equal levels, previous-day and previous-week extremes, session boundaries and range edges are marked before price arrives. A sweep is then classified by what price actually does—penetration, reclaim, acceptance, continuation or structural response—rather than by an assumed stop-hunt narrative.

The book explicitly rejects the idea that taking liquidity must cause reversal. Strong trends can consume one local pool and continue toward a larger external objective, and volatile or fragmented markets can print venue-specific extremes. Entry confirmation, invalidation, targets, risk, XAUUSD and index examples, market-specific considerations, bullish and bearish casebooks, journaling and replay convert the concept into a decision process that can retain failures and no-trade cases.

THE CHART-READING PROBLEM

Three similar-looking moves. Three very different decisions.

Do not let a dramatic wick make the decision for you. A prior high or low matters only in the context of what price does after it is crossed. Compare the evidence for rejection, acceptance and staying flat.

REJECTION

Sweep + reclaim

The market trades beyond a pre-marked level, then returns through it.

What to ask: Did price reclaim decisively? Is there a structural response, a technical invalidation and room to the next objective?

What it means: A reversal becomes a candidate, not a certainty. A breach alone is not an entry signal.

ACCEPTANCE

Breakout holds beyond the level

The market crosses the reference and continues to trade on the far side.

What to ask: Are later closes accepting the new area rather than rejecting it? Does the higher-timeframe context support continuation?

What it means: Automatically fading the break can be the wrong decision; the sweep narrative is not confirmed.

UNCLEAR

Ambiguous? Pass.

Price crosses a visible level, but reclaim, structure or execution conditions remain inconclusive.

What to ask: Is the reference meaningful? Is confirmation late, volatility excessive or the risk-to-target geometry unusable?

What it means: “No trade” is a valid classification. A beautiful wick can still be an expensive guess.

WHY THIS MATTERS ON REAL CHARTS

Two decisions traders often confuse

After a sell-side liquidity breach

Imagine a well-defined range low breaks. Some traders call it a bullish sweep immediately. The book asks what happened after the break: did price reclaim the boundary, show meaningful structural change, and leave a realistic route to opposing liquidity?

Decision: a long is merely a candidate until the planned gates are satisfied. A late reclaim or a stop that makes the trade impractical can still lead to pass.

After a buy-side liquidity breach

Price takes a previous-day high. An upper wick looks dramatic, but the next bars hold above the reference. Is the market rejecting the level or accepting prices beyond it? That distinction matters more than the wick's appearance.

Decision: do not force a short just because liquidity was crossed. Continuation and insufficient evidence belong in the classification system too.

TRADER QUESTIONS, WITHOUT THE HYPE

Frequently asked questions about Liquidity Sweep

Does a liquidity sweep always mean a reversal?

No. The book separates a breach and reclaim from continued acceptance beyond the level. A sweep can precede a reversal, continuation or no actionable trade.

What is the difference between a sweep and a breakout?

The decision depends on what happens after price breaches a pre-marked level: reclaim and rejection support one reading; sustained acceptance beyond the level supports another.

Does the book cover XAUUSD and other markets?

Yes. It discusses Forex, gold/XAUUSD, stock indices and cryptocurrency markets, including differences in volatility, sessions, spreads and leverage.

Does Liquidity Sweep include actual practice tools?

Yes. It includes a liquidity mapping worksheet, pre-trade decision sheet, sweep-versus-breakout diagnostic, risk sheet, journal, failed-trade review, backtesting record and 20-chart self-test.

How many chapters does the book contain?

The 151-page edition's table of contents lists 41 numbered chapters, followed by a field manual, a 20-chart self-test and a ten-infographic visual atlas.

Can a valid-looking sweep still lose?

Yes. The book studies beautiful-looking losers, false confirmation, failed sweeps, costly entries and no-trade decisions instead of filtering them out after the outcome.

Is this a guaranteed trading strategy?

No. This is educational analysis, not financial advice. Historical patterns and diagrammed scenarios do not guarantee future returns; execution costs, leverage and risk matter.

Market-structure dispatch · Reading the scene · Study 01/12

Why the Visible Level Matters More Than the Trading Myth

Liquidity is a location on a chart before it becomes a story about someone else's stops. A visible high or low can attract stop orders, breakout entries and passive liquidity at the same time. No candle can identify the full inventory of those orders. The practical advantage begins with drawing the reference before price approaches it.

A question to take to the chart: If you had to hide the next ten candles, which level would still be significant?

EDITORIAL VISUAL 01

The obvious high is a decision point: a rule worth testing before the next candle

A prior high is visible to many participants, but the candles alone cannot reveal which stop, breakout or resting orders are present. Marking the high before the approach makes later acceptance or rejection classifiable.

Practical test: timestamp the level and note the first close beyond it. Do not redraw the reference after the chart has moved.

The classification desk · Reversal versus continuation · Study 02/12

A Stop Run Does Not Tell You Which Side Wins

A temporary excursion beyond a level and a breakout that establishes trade beyond it may begin with remarkably similar price movement. The critical difference appears afterward: reclaim and rejection on one side, sustained acceptance and continuation on the other. Calling every breach a manipulation narrative removes the possibility of being wrong.

A question to take to the chart: What would convince you that the old boundary has stopped working?

Execution notebook · The second decision · Study 03/12

The Reclaim Test: What Happens After the Wick?

Reclaim is evidence that price has returned across a reference, but it may be immediate, delayed, hesitant or unsupported by structure. Its quality depends on how the book's chosen rules specify a valid close, available follow-through and setup expiry. A dramatic wick without those conditions is still an observation rather than an entry.

Build a Liquidity Map Before the Session Becomes Exciting

Useful mapping ranks obvious swing extremes, range edges and previous-day levels instead of turning every small pivot into a signal.

Session intelligence · A clock-aware approach · Study 05/12

Why Timing Changes a Liquidity Event

Asia, London and New York sessions do not necessarily offer the same participation, spreads or volatility. A prior session extreme can be useful because its construction is objective, but only once the trader defines the data source, timezone, daylight-saving policy and trading hours. Otherwise apparently identical backtests may examine different levels.
