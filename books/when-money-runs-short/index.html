About this book

When Money Runs Short is a practical decision guide for periods when income no longer covers the real cost of living and small frugality tips are not enough. It distinguishes three levels of response—saving, substitution, and changing the model of daily life—then applies them to food, housing, transport, healthcare, clothing, education, family life, debt, savings, and recovery. The book asks readers to separate needs from the expensive ways those needs are currently met, rank expenses by the harm caused if they disappear, protect a realistic dignity floor, and examine large structural costs before exhausting themselves over tiny purchases. Its workbook turns the framework into concrete tools for mapping the monthly shortfall, comparing the real cost of a car or home, calculating cost per meal and cost per use, reviewing automatic payments, mapping debt, defining emergency-reserve rules, and testing non-essential expenses through a thirty-day absence experiment.

What you will learn

  • Distinguish a small frugality problem from a structural monthly shortfall that requires larger changes.
  • Separate the underlying need from the current way of paying for or meeting that need.
  • Classify expenses by what breaks if they disappear rather than by price alone.
  • Protect health, safety, the ability to work, children’s future, and a realistic dignity floor while cutting costs.
  • Compare the true cost of food, housing, transport, automatic payments and ownership rather than relying on headline prices.
  • Use cost-per-meal, cost-per-use and total-cost thinking to compare alternatives more accurately.
  • Create clearer rules for emergency reserves, debt, savings and family decision-making under pressure.
  • Test whether a non-essential expense is truly needed by pausing it and observing the real consequences before deciding permanently.

Who this book is for

For general readers facing a period in which income no longer comfortably covers the real cost of living and who need to restructure essential and everyday expenses without causing unnecessary damage to health, safety, work, relationships, future capacity or self-respect.
