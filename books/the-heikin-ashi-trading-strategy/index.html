About this book

The Heikin Ashi Trading Strategy explains the transformed candle series as a smoothing and trend-reading tool while keeping it separate from the actual prices available for execution. A Heikin Ashi close is not the market close, and a visually clean transformed sequence can hide the size and volatility of the underlying bars. Entry, stop and backtest rules therefore have to reference executable market prices rather than assume the transformed chart itself can be traded.

The book also explains why session gaps can appear reduced or disappear in Heikin Ashi even though real orders can still gap and slip. Trend persistence, trigger definitions, volatility, position sizing, exposure, trade frequency and drawdown are treated together so a visually elegant chart does not create false confidence about execution or risk.

Real SPY prices versus calculated Heikin Ashi values

This book contains a particularly effective numerical lesson: the transformed candle can look convincing while displaying values that were never the market's actual open or close. The author calculates Heikin Ashi from documented historical SPY and QQQ daily OHLC observations rather than assuming the chart transformation is a tradable price series.

Actual SPY market bar: January 4, 2010

The source records Open 112.37, High 113.39, Low 111.51, Close 113.33 for the first SPY bar of its historical sample. These are market OHLC observations, not smoothed Heikin Ashi readings.

Heikin Ashi close is an average, not a fill

Using HA Close = (O + H + L + C) / 4 gives 112.65 (rounded to two decimals), even though the actual market closed at 113.33. The difference is approximately 0.68 per share.

The open uses a recursive state

For the first bar, the book documents the common initialization HA Open = (actual Open + actual Close) / 2 = 112.85. Subsequent HA opens depend on the previous HA open and HA close. This continuity is part of the mathematics, not evidence of more orderly transactions.

What this means for stops, gaps and backtesting

A strategy built on Heikin Ashi signals must map entries, stops, slippage, session gaps and P&L to actual executable market prices. The source warns that backtesting as if all transformed prices were directly tradable can create misleading results.

Book-based numerical decision: A trader observes the January 4 SPY Heikin Ashi close of 112.65 and attempts to use it as the market close for a fill calculation. But the source bar's ordinary close was 113.33. The chart signal may still be useful as a filter; the execution simulator must use the raw OHLC or more granular trading data. Confusing the two introduces a price assumption unsupported by the original observation.

Practical questions readers ask

Can I place a market order at the Heikin Ashi close?

No. Heikin Ashi close is a calculated indicator value, not a quote or guaranteed market transaction price.

Why does a Heikin Ashi trend sometimes look smoother than the ordinary candle chart?

The transformed close averages the current bar and the transformed open depends on previous transformed values. The visual persistence is partly a consequence of smoothing.

Do the examples use genuine historical observations?

The source identifies a public OHLC-Vol dataset for historical source bars and gives specific SPY and QQQ cases. The resulting Heikin Ashi chart is an analytical reconstruction, not a broker statement.
