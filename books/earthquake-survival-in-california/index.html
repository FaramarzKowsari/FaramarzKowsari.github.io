About this book

Earthquake Survival in California is a California-focused visual preparedness guide built around a simple idea: earthquakes cannot be scheduled, but safer choices can be prepared in advance. The book moves from the science of California earthquakes to practical household actions, protective behavior during shaking, the first critical minutes after the shaking stops, and the first days of recovery.

The opening sections explain why California shakes, the role of faults and plate motion, the difference between magnitude and intensity, and major fault systems including the San Andreas, Hayward, Garlock and San Jacinto faults. It also separates hazard from risk and shows how local soil, liquefaction, landslides, coastal tsunami exposure, fire following earthquakes, building age and building type can change the consequences of the same earthquake from one neighborhood to another.

The guide then turns preparedness into concrete household work. Readers are shown how to audit a home for falling hazards, anchor tall furniture, make bedrooms and kitchens safer, secure water heaters and major appliances, keep exits usable, prepare apartments and rental units, understand when professional structural evaluation is needed, protect documents, build a home inventory, review earthquake insurance, assemble go-bags and stay-at-home supplies, store emergency water, organize food, medicines and hygiene supplies, prepare for outages, enable earthquake alerts and build family communication plans.

During shaking, the book repeatedly centers the core protective action: Drop, Cover, and Hold On. Visual scenarios cover bedrooms, kitchens, bathrooms, living rooms, offices, classrooms, stores, stadiums, high-rise buildings, public transit, freeways, parking garages, outdoor locations and situations involving children, older adults or limited mobility.

The recovery sections address aftershocks, injury checks, first-aid priorities, gas and electrical hazards, water and plumbing concerns, evacuation decisions, safe exits, trapped-person situations, emergency calls, congested communications, official information, disrupted roads and transit, coastal tsunami evacuation, food and medicine continuity, sanitation, psychological first steps, support for children and vulnerable neighbors, careful re-entry and damage documentation. The final master-reference pages condense the book into checklists, a family plan, an emergency-kit packing guide, trusted information channels, transportation guidance and a before-during-after decision tree.

What you will learn

  • Understand the difference between earthquake hazard, exposure, vulnerability and actual risk.
  • Recognize how faults, soil conditions, liquefaction, landslides, tsunamis and post-earthquake fire can create different California risk profiles.
  • Use earthquake hazard information without treating a fault map as a precise damage forecast.
  • Perform a practical home safety audit and reduce nonstructural hazards from furniture, electronics, cabinets, glass and appliances.
  • Know when a problem can be handled with household preparedness and when structural questions require qualified professionals.
  • Build a go-bag, stay-at-home supply kit, water reserve, food plan, medical continuity kit and sanitation backup.
  • Enable California earthquake warnings and understand what early warning can and cannot do.
  • Create family communication, meeting-place, school pickup, accessibility and pet plans.
  • Practice Drop, Cover, and Hold On and adapt it to different environments and mobility needs.
  • Respond safely after shaking by checking injuries, fire, gas, electricity, structural hazards and aftershocks before moving unnecessarily.
  • Decide whether to stay or leave using immediate hazards, official instructions and household needs rather than panic.
  • Use official information, communicate efficiently when networks are congested and navigate disrupted roads or transit.
  • Manage the first 24–72 hours with water, food, medicines, hygiene, emotional support and neighborhood welfare checks.
  • Document damage, contact insurers, keep repair records and avoid unsafe re-entry or rushed repair decisions.

California-specific topics

  • San Andreas Fault and other major California fault systems
  • Bay Area and Southern California regional differences
  • Soft soils, liquefaction and earthquake-induced landslides
  • California coastal tsunami readiness
  • Soft-story and unreinforced masonry buildings
  • Renters, apartments and manufactured homes
  • MyShake, Android Earthquake Alerts and Wireless Emergency Alerts
  • Official information from local emergency authorities, Cal OES, USGS and California Geological Survey
  • California transportation disruption and post-earthquake route planning
  • Earthquake insurance, documentation and early recovery

Who this book is for

For California homeowners, renters, families, apartment residents, students, commuters, older adults, caregivers, people with disabilities, pet owners, teachers, workplace teams and anyone who wants a visual, practical framework for what to do before, during and after an earthquake.

How the guide is organized

  • Understanding risk: California geology, faults, shaking, soil, secondary hazards and building vulnerability.
  • Before the earthquake: home safety, supplies, documents, alerts, communication and household planning.
  • During the earthquake: Drop, Cover, and Hold On across common real-world settings.
  • Immediately after: injuries, aftershocks, utilities, evacuation, trapped people, communications and official information.
  • First 24–72 hours and early recovery: food, water, medicines, sanitation, mental health, children, neighbors and damage documentation.
  • California master reference: compact checklists and one-page decision tools for fast review.

Safety note

This is an educational preparedness guide. Earthquake conditions vary by location, building, infrastructure and individual needs. For structural questions, utility hazards, evacuation orders, medical emergencies and current hazard information, follow qualified professionals and official local, state and federal guidance.
