About this book

Earthquake Survival in California is a 120-step visual preparedness, protective-action, and early-recovery system built specifically for California residents and visitors. It begins with earthquake science and local risk literacy—faults, shaking, ground failure, liquefaction, landslides, tsunami, buildings, and the correct use of hazard maps—while repeatedly separating hazard from a prediction of damage at a specific address. California geography, housing, coasts, freeways, Bay Area and Southern California contexts are used only when they change the decision, so the book remains a practical safety guide rather than a collection of landmarks or generic disaster posters.

The preparation sections move room by room and household by household. Readers learn how to reduce nonstructural hazards, distinguish what can be secured personally from what requires qualified structural assessment, protect important documents, build a home inventory, understand the general earthquake-insurance coverage gap, prepare a portable go-bag and stay-at-home supplies, plan water and food, maintain medications and medical devices, organize sanitation, prepare for power outages, and build family and accessibility-aware plans. Current California warning channels—including MyShake, Android Earthquake Alerts, Wireless Emergency Alerts, and the broader ShakeAlert ecosystem—are placed inside the larger rule that a warning is a cue for protective action, not a prediction tool.

The action and recovery sections cover Drop, Cover, and Hold On; bedrooms, high-rises, schools, workplaces, stores, outdoor spaces, freeways and other transportation contexts; coastal tsunami natural warnings; aftershocks; injury and household checks; fire, gas and electrical hazards; evacuation; communications; first 24–72-hour needs; damage documentation; cautious re-entry; professional assessment; and early recovery. The final master-reference pages compress the system into memorable before, during, after, 72-hour and recovery decisions. The book’s underlying message is practical rather than alarmist: uncertainty cannot be eliminated, but rehearsed decisions, safer spaces, reliable information, and prepared supplies can create better choices when time is short.

What you will learn

  • Understand California earthquake hazards without confusing hazard maps, regulatory zones, or historical patterns with address-specific damage predictions.
  • Reduce household nonstructural hazards while recognizing when structural assessment or retrofit design requires qualified professionals.
  • Prepare documents, home inventory, insurance questions, go-bags, stay-at-home supplies, water, food, medicines, sanitation, and backup power.
  • Set up and understand California earthquake warning channels such as MyShake, Android Earthquake Alerts, WEA, and ShakeAlert.
  • Apply Drop, Cover, and Hold On and adapt protective action to bedrooms, high-rises, schools, workplaces, stores, outdoors, and transportation.
  • Make safer driving decisions on California roads and freeways during and after shaking without assuming bridges or routes remain open.
  • Recognize tsunami as a conditional coastal hazard and respond to strong or long shaking and official or natural warnings appropriately.
  • Check people, fire, gas, electricity, building hazards, and aftershocks conservatively after shaking.
  • Plan the first 24–72 hours around water, information, shelter, communication, accessibility, and household continuity.
  • Document damage, approach re-entry cautiously, use qualified assessment, verify official assistance, and move into early recovery methodically.
