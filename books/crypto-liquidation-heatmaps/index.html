About this book

Crypto Liquidation Heatmaps explains what a liquidation heatmap can and cannot tell a trader. It separates realized liquidations from model-projected liquidation levels and from ordinary order-book liquidity, then examines how heatmap providers construct estimates from leverage assumptions, price paths and incomplete public information. Bright clusters are treated as conditional stress zones rather than guaranteed magnets or future price targets.

The book develops a full trading workflow around cluster distance, density, open interest, funding, volume and CVD, market structure, cascades, continuation and reversal hypotheses. It includes setup logic, no-trade conditions, stops, position sizing, case studies, a checklist, journaling and playbook construction. The aim is to use heatmaps as one evidence layer in a risk-controlled process while keeping model uncertainty visible.

Featured Medium Article: The Heatmap Moved. Bitcoin Didn't. So What Actually Changed?

Can a liquidation heatmap change dramatically even when Bitcoin's price barely moves? In his new Medium feature, The Heatmap Moved. Bitcoin Didn't. So What Actually Changed?, author Faramarz Kowsari begins with this apparent contradiction and explores the changing, partially hidden landscape of leveraged cryptocurrency positions. A liquidation heatmap is not a fixed itinerary for price: its projected stress zones can change as positions are opened or closed, leverage and collateral conditions evolve, and providers update their models.

The essay distinguishes realized liquidation events, projected liquidation levels, and displayed order-book liquidity, explaining why these are different forms of evidence rather than interchangeable signals. It considers how liquidation cascades and sweep-and-reclaim reversals can emerge from different market conditions, and why a bright cluster does not guarantee that price will reach it or react in a particular way.

Moving beyond the heatmap itself, the article examines open interest, funding rates, volume, cumulative volume delta (CVD), and market structure as complementary analytical lenses. It also explores the psychology of false precision, the limits of model assumptions, and a disciplined decision process that allows for explicit invalidation, position-risk controls, and a no-trade outcome when evidence is inconclusive.
