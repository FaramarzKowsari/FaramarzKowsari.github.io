About this book

Mastering Support & Resistance is designed as an 80-page visual field guide for turning vague horizontal lines into usable trading decisions. It teaches the reader to identify a meaningful level, convert the line into a realistic zone, wait for price behavior at that area and enter only when the planned confirmation and invalidation logic are present. The book treats support and resistance as maps of probability rather than guaranteed predictions.

The visual lessons cover bounces, entries, exits, breakouts, retests, failed breaks and traps with candlestick examples, support and resistance zones, supply and demand context, liquidity references, checklists and risk reminders. Its practical emphasis is on reading reaction first, separating a level from a signal and making the trade plan clear enough that patience, confirmation and risk can override the temptation to buy or sell blindly at a line.

Inside the book: a source-based learning route

These pathways explain how the material develops. The thematic grouping is editorial navigation for the 80 visual pages, not a claim that the original has eight formal parts.

Visual theme 1: The foundation of a useful level

Supply and demand, structural swing highs and lows, market memory, round numbers, zones rather than precise lines, and how wick and candle-body information differ.

Visual theme 2: Judge level quality

Clean reactions versus chop, frequency and recency of tests, fresh versus exhausted levels, zone width and the role of volatility. A level with many touches is not automatically stronger.

Visual theme 3: When support and resistance exchange roles

Support-to-resistance flips, resistance-to-support flips, first retests, sustained body closes, false breaks and why the opposite outcome remains possible.

Visual theme 4: Practice the four common trade decisions

Bounce from support, rejection from resistance, breakout and continuation, and fake-breakout reversal. The infographics put invalidation and risk beside the chart.

Visual theme 5: Ranges, compression and stops

Range floors and ceilings, consolidation boxes, liquidity beyond obvious highs and lows, sweeps, reclaim behavior and waiting for sufficient confirmation.

Visual theme 6: Confirmation with context

Volume, trendlines, moving averages, VWAP, Fibonacci overlaps, pivot levels and multi-timeframe alignment are used as supporting evidence rather than automatic trading signals.

Visual theme 7: Build a cleaner chart routine

Choose the timeframe, mark only meaningful zones, review why price reacted, state a stop and target, and keep chart images for later comparison.

Visual theme 8: Replay, inspect and improve

Use each standalone visual page as a practice card: cover the labels, decide what the chart permits and compare your thinking with the annotated process.

Practical decision checklist

  1. Define the active context before selecting the apparent setup.
  2. Choose only levels or signals that fit the described conditions.
  3. Identify what the next market observation would confirm or contradict.
  4. Write the exact invalidation and defensible next objective.
  5. Check transaction costs, exposure and execution risks.
  6. Record false signals and no-trade cases as carefully as successful reactions.

Questions before buying

Is this book mainly visual?

Yes. It is organized around eighty practical infographics with annotated price charts, comparisons, checklists and examples.

Are levels better drawn as zones than as exact prices?

The guide emphasizes zones and explains why candle bodies, wicks, instrument volatility and repeated interactions affect boundaries.

Are false breakouts included?

Yes. Several illustrated pages compare real and failed breakouts, first retests and trap patterns.

Are other technical tools included?

The visuals cover volume, VWAP, Fibonacci, trendlines, moving averages and pivot references alongside classical support and resistance.

Do strong levels guarantee a bounce?

No. Visual strength scores and filters in the guide are educational heuristics; no single level has a guaranteed reaction.
