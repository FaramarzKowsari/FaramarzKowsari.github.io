About this book

The 1-Minute & 5-Minute Scalping Playbook is built around a strict division of labor between timeframes: the five-minute chart supplies context, structure and location, while the one-minute chart is primarily an execution lens. It covers the cost of trading fast, liquidity and volatility, market structure, session mapping, no-trade conditions, trigger quality, candle sequences, precision entries and stop placement before introducing eight distinct playbooks.

The second half turns setups into a repeatable operating process through reward-to-risk, position sizing, overtrading controls, daily loss limits, abnormal-news conditions, pre-market routines, journaling, meaningful metrics, backtesting and replay. Complete case sets include good trades, bad trades and losing trades that still followed the rules, followed by a personal 1M/5M playbook, one-page trading plan, 30-session development program and practical appendices for sizing, R-multiples, checklists and review.

The five-minute map. The one-minute trigger. One accountable decision.

A fast chart can tempt a trader to make a decision before the context is clear. This book assigns different jobs to two timeframes: the five-minute chart defines structure, location and acceptable market states; the one-minute chart is used mainly to execute a scenario already planned. Rather than promising quick profit, it links entry timing to spreads, slippage, limits, position size and an honest record of what happened after the click.

Inside the book: a source-based learning route

These pathways explain how the material develops. The chapters and topics follow the manuscript rather than a collection of generic trading terms.

Chapters 1–8: Market state before momentum

Start with what scalping is, why the costs of fast trading matter, the role of liquidity and volatility, session mapping, five-minute structure and a no-trade map that can veto a seemingly attractive signal.

Chapters 9–12: Trigger quality and entry precision

Study one-minute candle sequences, the difference between location and trigger, entry without chasing, and stops placed at a genuine invalidation rather than an arbitrary distance.

Chapters 13–20: Eight named playbooks

Practice five-minute trend / one-minute pullback; sweep and reclaim; breakout, retest and continuation; failed breakout reversal; opening-range momentum; VWAP reclaim and rejection; compression to expansion; and five-minute reversal with one-minute execution.

Chapters 21–26: Fast trade management

Targets, R-multiples, position sizing, the overtrading trap, daily loss limits, news spikes and abnormal conditions: small moves make the practical cost of execution exceptionally important.

Chapters 27–33: Prepare, test and review

A fifteen-minute premarket routine, live checklists, trade journaling, useful metrics, honest 1M/5M backtesting, replay trading and transitioning cautiously from simulation to execution.

Chapters 34–40 and appendices: The practice laboratory

Eight complete five-minute scalps; eight one-minute execution studies; trades that should not have been taken; losing trades that followed rules; a personal playbook; one-page plan; and a thirty-session development program.

Where the method meets a difficult chart

The trend looks clear but the 1M entry is late

A correct five-minute bias is not permission to chase a one-minute candle far from invalidation. Look again at location, the attainable target and the actual risk.

A beautiful setup during a volatility spike

If spreads or fills become unpredictable, a textbook candle can be a poor executable trade. A no-trade decision deserves to be recorded.

The well-executed losing trade

The book separates the quality of an original decision from its result; a stop can be hit without making every component of the plan wrong.

Practical decision checklist

  1. Define the active context before selecting the apparent setup.
  2. Choose only levels or signals that fit the described conditions.
  3. Identify what the next market observation would confirm or contradict.
  4. Write the exact invalidation and defensible next objective.
  5. Check transaction costs, exposure and execution risks.
  6. Record false signals and no-trade cases as carefully as successful reactions.

Questions before buying

How are the two timeframes used?

Five-minute structure provides context and location; one-minute price action helps time an entry only after the scenario is defined.

How many entry playbooks are included?

Eight named playbooks are developed in Chapters 13 through 20, with further case sets later in the book.

Does it cover fees and slippage?

Yes. The manuscript explicitly emphasizes transaction costs, liquidity and execution uncertainty because scalping targets short moves.

Are losing trades and mistakes included?

Yes. Dedicated chapters examine trades that should never have been opened as well as losing trades that followed the rules.

Is there a systematic practice plan?

The manuscript includes a thirty-session development program, chart drills, a journal template and a one-page trading plan.
