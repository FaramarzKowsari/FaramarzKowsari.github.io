About this book

Weather, Air Quality and Extreme Heat at Home is a 100-page visual handbook built around one practical idea: a home is a connected heat-and-air system, not a collection of unrelated devices. The book begins by showing how heat enters, is generated, is stored, moves through materials and air, and eventually must leave. Radiation, conduction, convection, thermal mass, humidity, air movement, hot nights, urban heat islands, and the microclimate outside a particular window are translated into household-scale diagrams so readers can diagnose why a room or dwelling becomes uncomfortable before buying a solution.

The middle of the book turns diagnosis into layered action. It examines window orientation and glass, roofs, walls, insulation, thermal bridges, leakage, room layout, internal heat sources, balconies and top-floor conditions, then develops a shade-first strategy using external shading, interior blinds and curtains, solar-control films, reflective surfaces, trees, cool roofs, and rental-friendly measures. Ventilation is treated as a decision rather than a ritual: cross-ventilation, single-sided ventilation, stack effect, night flushing, fan placement, ceiling fans, evaporative cooling, dehumidification, zone cooling, air-conditioning types and sizing, thermostat strategy, portable units, maintenance, and smart controls are compared according to context.

Heat is only half the indoor-environment problem, so the later pages connect cooling to air quality and energy. The book covers outdoor pollution, PM2.5, wildfire smoke, the trade-off between opening windows for cooling and closing them for cleaner air, air purifiers, clean-air rooms, CO₂ as a ventilation clue, cooking particles and moisture, condensation and mold risk, electricity bills, cooling demand, cost-versus-impact decisions, and power-outage strategies. Illustrative household scenarios—including Mediterranean and humid coastal homes—show how the pieces interact, and the final action planning converts the whole system into a practical 24-hour routine that changes with sun, temperature, humidity, air quality, and household needs.

What you will learn

  • Read a home as a heat-and-air system and identify how radiation, conduction, convection, internal gains, storage, and leakage interact.
  • Build a simple home heat profile using orientation, windows, roof, walls, insulation, layout, occupancy, and local microclimate.
  • Prioritize shading and solar control before relying only on mechanical cooling.
  • Decide when ventilation can help by comparing indoor and outdoor temperature, humidity, air quality, wind, noise, security, and time of day.
  • Use cross-ventilation, night flushing, fans, ceiling fans, zone cooling, and other low-energy strategies in the right context.
  • Understand the roles and limitations of evaporative cooling, dehumidification, portable air conditioners, and correctly sized cooling systems.
  • Balance heat relief with PM2.5, wildfire smoke, outdoor pollution, filtration, and clean-air-room strategies.
  • Use CO₂ as a ventilation clue without treating it as a universal measure of every indoor pollutant.
  • Connect cooling choices to electricity use, maintenance, cost, resilience, and power-outage planning.
  • Create a 24-hour household heat plan that changes as conditions change rather than following one fixed rule.

Who this book is for

For renters and homeowners, apartment residents, households facing hot summers or heat waves, people balancing cooling with indoor-air concerns, and non-specialist readers who want a practical visual framework for making their home cooler, cleaner, and more energy-aware without assuming one solution fits every building or climate.
