A complete visual A1 Turkish course in one volume

Turkish A1 Visual Grammar brings Books 1-5 plus the A1 Booster together in a 307-page illustrated course designed to help beginners move from first words and basic sentence patterns toward practical everyday Turkish. Grammar is presented visually, with clear examples, recurring characters, compact explanations, story scenes and cumulative practice so learners can see how Turkish words are built and how suffixes change meaning.

The collection develops the Turkish alphabet and vowel harmony, nominal sentences, possession, var / yok, common verbs, present continuous, location and movement cases, time expressions, daily routines, simple past, nominal past and future forms, commands and suggestions, wants and preferences, connectors, practical shopping language, definite-object marking, noun compounds, spatial expressions, word order, adverbs, comparison and -ki. Review charts, mini-stories, challenge pages and integrated quests repeatedly bring earlier grammar back into use.

Book 1 — A1 foundations

  • Greetings such as Merhaba, Günaydın, İyi akşamlar and Görüşürüz.
  • The Turkish alphabet, including special letters such as Ç, Ğ, I, İ, Ö and Ü.
  • Vowel harmony and the distinction between back/front vowel groups.
  • Personal pronouns: ben, sen, o, biz, siz, onlar.
  • Plural formation with -lar / -ler.
  • Demonstratives bu, şu, o and distance-based use.
  • Question particles mı / mi / mu / mü.
  • Numbers 1-10 and basic beginner review activities.

Book 2 — Nominal sentences, possession and existence

  • Nominal sentences such as Ben öğrenciyim, Sen öğretmensin and Biz arkadaşız.
  • Personal endings and vowel harmony in identity sentences.
  • Negation with değil and questions such as Sen öğrenci misin?
  • Simple demonstrative sentences: Bu ev, Şu okul, Bu ne?
  • Adjective + noun order, including examples such as büyük ev and kırmızı araba.
  • Possessive endings for my, your, his/her, our, your and their.
  • Everyday possession and family language such as kitabım, kedin, evi, okulumuz.
  • Var / yok for existence and possession, including classroom, room, family and café contexts.
  • Short visual mini-stories that combine identity, possession and existence in context.

Book 3 — Actions, movement and time

  • Turkish infinitives with -mak / -mek and common verbs such as okumak, yazmak, yemek, içmek.
  • Present continuous with -yor, including person forms such as okuyorum, yazıyorsun, geliyorlar.
  • Negative present continuous patterns such as uyumuyorum and çalışmıyoruz.
  • Present-continuous questions with -yor mu? and question words such as ne, nereye, kim.
  • Directional case system: locative -da/-de/-ta/-te, dative -a/-e/-ya/-ye, and ablative -dan/-den/-tan/-ten.
  • Hard-consonant harmony and consonant softening in case formation.
  • Movement and location sentences such as Eve gidiyorum, Evde oturuyorum and Evden çıkıyorum.
  • Telling time, half-hours, days of the week, dün / bugün / yarın, and time-of-day vocabulary.
  • Daily-routine sequences including waking up, breakfast, going to work, working, resting and sleeping.

Book 4 — The Past & The Future

Book 4 turns the A1 course into a time-travel grammar sequence. The first part teaches the Turkish simple past with -DI, including vowel harmony in -dı/-di/-du/-dü, the hard-consonant variants -tı/-ti/-tu/-tü, personal endings, negative forms and questions. Examples such as Ben geldim, Sen gittin, O okudu, Biz izledik, Gelmedim, Geldin mi? and Ne yaptın? make completed past actions visible as word-building patterns.

The course then moves to nominal past: saying that someone or something was a student, child, sick, beautiful or at home. Learners see forms such as öğrenciydim, çocuktun, hava güzeldi, hastaydım and evdeydim, together with negative değildi, questions such as Güzel miydi?, and past existence/possession through vardı / yoktu, including Arabam vardı and Zamanım yoktu.

The final sequence introduces the future tense with -ECEK / -ACAK, vowel harmony, buffer y, the k → ğ change before vowel-initial personal endings, negative future and future questions. Forms such as gideceğim, yapacaksın, okuyacak, yiyeceğiz, gelmeyeceğim and Gelecek misin? are tied to time expressions such as yarın, haftaya, gelecek yıl, dün and geçen yıl. A three-part yesterday/today/tomorrow story and a final review challenge connect past and future in context.

Book 5 — Commands, desires, connectors & practical shopping

Book 5 is the A1 finale. It begins with the imperative: direct commands such as Gel! and Git!, negative commands with -ma/-me such as Yapma!, and plural/formal commands such as Gelin / Geliniz. It then introduces inclusive suggestions with -alım / -elim, including Gidelim! and Yapalım!, so learners can move from telling someone what to do to proposing an action together.

The next stage teaches verb + istemek for wants and intentions: Gitmek istiyorum, Uyumak istiyorum and Yemek istiyor musun?. Sentence linking expands through ve, ama/fakat, veya/ya da and çünkü, while practical relation words add ile/-la/-le, için, gibi and kadar.

A Grand Bazaar sequence turns the grammar into everyday shopping language: Ne kadar?, large-number money review, çok pahalı / çok ucuz, Bunu alıyorum, and ordinal forms such as birinci, ikinci and sonuncu. Four dialogue scenes combine earlier A1 material for meeting people, ordering food, asking directions and making plans. The final multi-stage review revisits identity, plurals, present/past/future verbs, location cases and wants before a complete A1 skill-tree recap.

A1 Booster — Cases, compounds, word order & final consolidation

The A1 Booster extends the core series with a concentrated visual review of structures that are especially useful before moving into A2. It opens with the definite object / accusative case -ı / -i / -u / -ü, contrasting Kitap okuyorum with Kitabı okuyorum, then adds vowel harmony and buffer y in forms such as arabayı, kediyi and suyu. Questions such as Kitabı görüyor musun? reinforce the difference between a general and a specific object.

The next sections cover noun compounds such as Türk kahvesi, okul çantası, telefon numarası and ev kapısı, followed by personal possession such as benim okul çantam. Spatial language is built with üstünde, altında, yanında, içinde, önünde and arkasında, using examples like masanın üstünde, evin yanında and kutunun içinde.

Sentence building becomes more systematic through the visual order Subject + Time + Place + Object + Verb, with examples such as Ben bugün evde kitap okuyorum. Learners also practice common adverbs including çok, az, biraz, hemen, şimdi, sonra and önce; comparison with daha; superlatives with en; and -ki in location modifiers and ownership forms such as evdeki kitap, masadaki telefon, benimki and seninki. A question-word review, integrated mini-stories, review chart and test arena bring these structures together as a bridge into A2.

What learners practice

  • Recognizing Turkish suffix patterns through repeated visual examples and word-by-word breakdowns.
  • Building complete beginner sentences instead of memorizing isolated vocabulary.
  • Using vowel harmony to choose plural, personal, possessive, tense and case endings.
  • Moving between present, simple past, nominal past and future forms in short everyday narratives.
  • Using commands, negative commands, formal/plural imperatives and -alım/-elim suggestions.
  • Expressing wants with -mak/-mek + istemek and linking ideas with common conjunctions.
  • Handling practical shopping language, prices, inexpensive/expensive comparisons and ordinal numbers.
  • Distinguishing general and specific objects with the accusative case.
  • Building noun compounds, spatial expressions and natural Turkish word order.
  • Using adverbs, daha, en and -ki in short real-life sentences.
  • Asking and answering yes/no, place, direction, time, quantity and ownership questions.
  • Reading dialogues and mini-stories with English support and reviewing grammar through charts, quests and challenge pages.

Who this book is for

For complete beginners, self-learners and A1 students who prefer visual explanations, repeated pattern recognition and practical sentence building. It is especially suited to learners who find conventional grammar-heavy textbooks too abstract and want to see each Turkish form broken into roots, suffixes and real examples before using it in dialogues, stories and everyday situations.
