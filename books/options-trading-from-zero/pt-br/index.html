Descrição pública original do livro

inglês

OPTIONS TRADING FROM ZERO is a structured beginner-to-intermediate guide that teaches options from the contract upward rather than from strategy names downward. The book begins with stocks, ETFs, calls, puts, rights and obligations, then builds the four basic option positions carefully so a new trader can distinguish buying from writing, directional exposure from contractual obligation, and limited-loss structures from positions that can carry much larger risk. Contract specifications, multipliers, premium, moneyness, intrinsic and extrinsic value, breakeven, expiration, exercise, assignment, settlement, dividends, early assignment and pin risk are treated as operational facts that must be understood before a strategy is placed. The next stage turns the options chain into a readable decision tool. The book explains how equity, ETF and index options differ, why broker permissions and collateral matter, and how to interpret strike, expiry, bid, ask, last price, volume, open interest and implied volatility without confusing displayed information with an executable trade. Liquidity, spread width, order type, hidden friction and strike/expiration selection are integrated into the same workflow. The emphasis is practical: a theoretical payoff is not enough if the contract is illiquid, the order cannot fill near the assumed price, or the trader does not understand the settlement and assignment rules attached to that product. A full section develops the pricing logic behind option premiums. Historical volatility, implied volatility, event premiums, delta, gamma, theta, vega, rho, skew, term structure and volatility crush are introduced as changing risk sensitivities rather than fixed predictions. Objetivos de aprendizagem publicados

inglês

  • Understand what a call and a put actually represent, including the rights of the buyer and the obligations of the writer.
  • Distinguish the four basic option positions and identify which structures have limited loss, substantial loss potential or assignment obligations.
  • Read contract specifications correctly, including strike, expiration, multiplier, settlement and product-specific rules before placing a trade.
  • Break option premium into intrinsic and extrinsic value and calculate breakeven instead of relying on visual payoff intuition alone.
  • Understand closing, exercising, assignment and settlement as different actions with different operational consequences.
  • Recognize early-assignment, dividend and expiration risks, including pin risk near the strike.
  • Read an option chain field by field and separate last price, bid, ask, volume, open interest and implied volatility.
  • Evaluate liquidity using spread quality and executable prices rather than assuming the displayed last trade is available.
