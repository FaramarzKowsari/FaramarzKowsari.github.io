Descripción pública original del libro

inglés

THE NARCISSISM SURVIVAL GUIDE is a practical guide to recognizing difficult narcissistic patterns without turning popular psychology labels into amateur diagnosis. It begins by separating narcissistic traits, presentations, interpersonal behavior and narcissistic personality disorder, then shifts the reader's attention toward what can actually be observed: entitlement, double standards, exploitation, image management, blame-shifting, gaslighting, humiliation, punitive silence, third-party pressure, threats, coercion and retaliation. The central question is not simply “What kind of person is this?” but “What is happening, what power does this person hold, what are the consequences, and what can I safely control?” The family chapters examine controlling parents, adult children and intrusive parents, siblings and in-laws, financial pressure, family alliances, aging, caregiving and vulnerability. The relationship chapters move from early dating signals and safer commitment decisions to living with a controlling partner, money and housing dependence, intimacy, digital control, children between parents, separation, divorce and post-breakup safety. Throughout these situations, the book treats dependency and power as practical variables rather than assuming that the same communication technique is safe in every relationship. A substantial workplace and institutional section extends the framework beyond romantic or family relationships. It covers humiliating bosses, manipulative coworkers, difficult employees, clients and business partners, evidence and HR procedures, teachers and mentors, banks and government offices, landlords, healthcare and essential services. Objetivos de aprendizaje publicados

inglés

  • Distinguish narcissistic traits and harmful interpersonal patterns from a clinical diagnosis of narcissistic personality disorder.
  • Replace subtype labels and mind-reading with a behavior map based on observable conduct, context, power, duration and consequences.
  • Recognize entitlement, double standards, gaslighting, blame-shifting, contempt, punitive silence, coercion and retaliation without treating any single behavior as diagnostic proof.
  • Assess how money, housing, employment, education, caregiving, immigration status, children and institutional authority can change the practical options available in a conflict.
  • Adapt boundaries to family, dating, partnership, workplace, education, business, public-service and social-media contexts instead of using one universal script.
  • State boundaries in terms of one's own decisions and consequences rather than attempts to control another person's personality.
  • Use brief, context-sensitive responses when speaking is safe and identify when further explanation is no longer productive.
  • Document incidents accurately by separating observable facts from interpretation and by defining the practical remedy or review being sought.
