Options Trading Books: 0DTE, Gamma, Flow & Volatility

Options introduce dimensions that do not exist in a simple directional trade: expiration, strike selection, implied volatility, liquidity and the Greeks. This collection ranges from calls, puts and option chains to 0DTE trading, gamma exposure and options flow. It is built for readers who want to understand why being correct about direction is not always enough, how time and volatility reshape a position, and how dealer positioning or unusual activity should be interpreted within the mechanics of the option itself.

0DTE Options: Trading the Same-Day Market

A Practical Guide to Same-Day Options, Greeks, Market Structure, Execution, Risk Control, and High-Speed Decision-Making

0DTE Options: Trading the Same-Day Market explains how same-day expiration compresses option mechanics, market movement, execution risk and behavioral pressure into a very short decision horizon. The first part builds the language of 0DTE and the Greeks; the middle converts those mechanics into structures and market contexts; the final part integrates execution, risk, evidence and trader behavior rather than treating them as separate subjects.

Gamma Exposure Trading

GEX, Dealer Positioning, Gamma Flip, Call Walls, Put Walls, 0DTE Options, and Intraday Market Structure

Gamma Exposure Trading explains the options-market layer that can influence intraday behavior in indexes, ETFs and futures without presenting GEX as a crystal ball. It starts with the minimum options knowledge needed to understand Delta, Gamma and position-scaled Gamma Exposure, then separates observed option-chain data from inferred dealer positioning. Open interest, static versus intraday estimates, vendor methodology and sign conventions are treated as model inputs with limitations rather than direct observations of every dealer book. The central discipline is conditional: build a map, wait for price to reach a relevant area, then let live market behavior confirm or reject the hypothesis.

Options Flow Trading

Unusual Options Activity, Sweeps, Blocks, Open Interest, Premium Flow, and Institutional Positioning

Options Flow Trading teaches readers to interpret listed-options transaction activity as evidence rather than as a shortcut to hidden institutional intent. The book starts with the mechanics required to read a flow feed responsibly: how an options order reaches the tape, how execution near the bid, ask or mid can be classified, why aggressor labels remain imperfect, and how sweeps, blocks and large prints should be understood without assuming that size or urgency automatically reveals direction.

VOLATILITY TRADING FROM ZERO

Understanding VIX, Implied Volatility, Skew, Term Structure, Volatility Risk Premium, and Trading Strategies

A trader can forecast a large market move and still lose money on a purchased option. A trader can correctly expect a rise in the VIX and still misunderstand the instrument held. VOLATILITY TRADING FROM ZERO begins with this uncomfortable but essential distinction: volatility is about the magnitude and distribution of returns, not simply whether prices rise or fall. The opening chapters build the mathematics of realized volatility, return measurement and time scaling, then connect option premium to implied volatility and the Greeks. This foundation explains why market direction, option price, the passage of time and changes in volatility expectations can disagree with one another.
